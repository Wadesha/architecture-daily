江汉关大楼

Jianghan Customs House

级别：国家级重点文物保护单位 年代：1924年建成 地址：武汉市江岸区沿江大道88号 类型：市政建筑·钢筋混凝土结构

江汉关大楼矗立于汉口长江之滨，是江汉路步行街的起点，也是汉口近代建筑群中最具标志性的一座。大楼由上海斯蒂华达生·斯贝司（Stewardson）建筑公司的英国建筑师辛浦生（Simpson）设计，景明洋行监工，魏清记营造厂施工，1922年动工，1924年建成，耗银200万两。建筑外观模仿英国伦敦国会大厦（威斯敏斯特宫）的样式，整体为钢筋混凝土结构，主楼四层，钟楼八层，建筑面积四千余平方米，是新古典主义与文艺复兴风格在汉口的典型实例。

大楼平面呈三合院式布局，正面沿江汉路、垂直于沿江大道，采用三段式构图。由于沿江大道在这里恰好拐了一个弯，大楼与沿江大道上其他并列的建筑相比，显得格外宏伟；从长江乘船来汉口的旅客，在江面上最先望见的就是江汉关钟楼——这是当年汉口的城市天际线标志。主入口不设在一层而是设在二层，门前有二十八级台阶，气势轩昂；这一设计一方面是为了仪式感，另一方面是出于对长江水患的充分考虑——汉口地势低洼，汛期江水常漫上街道，把入口抬高二层可以确保海关业务在洪水期间照常运行。门廊采用改良的八根科林斯柱式，四层通高，其中入口两侧为一对双柱，柱身比例修长，柱头茛苕叶卷舒自然。外墙和柱子全部用大件花岗岩建造，石质粗粝厚重，与文艺复兴式样相得益彰。

正中的钟楼高约46米，自下而上依次为摆陀室、机件室、音响室三层，内有钢梯盘旋通向顶层。四面均悬挂大钟，钟面直径达4米，钟声可传数里之外。站在钟楼上俯瞰，武汉三镇、长江汉水尽收眼底。江汉关的前身可追溯到1861年汉口开埠：当时各国商船在上海报关纳税后，即在汉口码头随意装卸货物，贸易买卖，甚至走私偷渡，均无人管束；经中国政府敦促，由英国领事官员牵头设立海关关卡，即为江汉关的滥觞。此后江汉关税务司长期由英国人担任，钟楼每天按时奏报旋律为《女王万岁》；文化大革命中改为《东方红》；1986年又重新恢复原旋律。一座钟楼的报时曲，浓缩了汉口自开埠以来一个多世纪的沧桑。2001年，江汉关大楼被国务院公布为第五批全国重点文物保护单位。

— 英文 English —

The Jianghan Customs House stands by the Yangtze in Hankou, marking the starting point of Jianghan Road Pedestrian Street and serving as the most iconic structure of Hankou's modern architectural ensemble. Designed by British architect Simpson of the Shanghai Stewardson firm, supervised by Hemmings & Berkley, and constructed by Wei Qingji Construction Company, it broke ground in 1922 and was completed in 1924 at a cost of 2 million taels of silver. Modeled after the Palace of Westminster in London, it is a reinforced concrete structure with a 4-story main building and 8-story clock tower, covering over 4,000 square meters — a classic example of Neoclassical and Renaissance style in Hankou.

The plan is U-shaped, with the main facade along Jianghan Road, perpendicular to Yanjiang Avenue, in a three-part composition. Because Yanjiang Avenue curves here, the building appears especially grand compared to adjacent structures; travelers approaching Hankou by boat along the Yangtze would see the Customs House clock tower first — the skyline landmark of old Hankou. The main entrance is set on the second floor, approached by 28 grand steps; this design served both ceremony and flood protection, as Hankou's low-lying site was prone to Yangtze flooding. Eight modified Corinthian columns rise four stories, with paired columns flanking the entrance. The exterior walls and columns are entirely clad in large granite blocks.

The central clock tower rises about 46 meters, housing the pendulum room, mechanism room, and chime room from bottom to top, with a steel ladder to the top bearing four clock faces 4 meters in diameter. From the tower, the three towns of Wuhan and the Yangtze and Han rivers are all in view. The Customs House originated in 1861 when Hankou was opened as a treaty port; foreign ships paid duties in Shanghai and then loaded and unloaded freely at Hankou, with smuggling rampant and no oversight. After Chinese government protests, British consular officials established a customs post — the precursor of Jianghan Customs. The clock long played "God Save the Queen" daily; during the Cultural Revolution it was changed to "The East is Red"; in 1986 the original melody was restored. One clock tower's chime thus encapsulates Hankou's century of change since opening. In 2001 it was designated a National Key Cultural Heritage Site.

大水井古建筑群

Dashuijing Ancient Building Complex

级别：国家级重点文物保护单位 年代：明末至民国 地址：恩施州利川市柏杨坝镇水井村、高仰村 类型：居住建筑·砖木结构

大水井古建筑群坐落于利川市柏杨坝镇水井村的半山腰上，南距利川城区45公里，东北距重庆奉节约100公里，北望齐岳大山，南靠寒池高岭，东揽尖刀古观，西控九龙雄关，四周群山环抱，地势险峻。整座建筑群由李氏宗祠、李亮清庄园和高仰台李盖五旧宅三大部分组成，占地总面积2万余平方米，建筑总面积1.2万余平方米，是鄂西南地区目前规模最大、艺术价值极高的古建筑群，被誉为"一首凝固在三峡南岸大巴山麓的建筑史诗"。2001年被国务院公布为第五批全国重点文物保护单位。

相传大水井宗祠、庄园原为土家族黄氏土司的住宅及祖墓所在地。清乾隆中期，湖南岳州年成歉收，李氏高祖李廷龙、李廷凤兄弟西迁谋生，李廷龙落业大水井，李廷凤落业云阳马鞍山，当地遂有"龙归井，凤栖山"之说。李廷龙初为黄氏佣工，种地煮酒，辛苦经营；后黄氏败落，李氏借管钱粮之机内贪外刮，逐渐蚕食原主家产，终成川鄂边境上的巨富。此后李氏子孙繁衍六代，由明末直至1949年，前后长达三百余年，先后建成宗祠、庄园与高仰台住宅，集不同历史时期的建筑风格于一身。

李氏宗祠依山就势建于水井村罗汉山山阴，坐南朝北，清道光二十六年（1846）兴工，二十九年（1849）告竣，在原土家人黄氏老宅和墓地基础上建成，建筑面积3800余平方米。宗祠为宫殿式建筑，中轴对称布局，三路三进院落，前壁开左、中、右三门；中轴线上主体建筑为前殿、拜殿、祖宗殿，三大殿形制相同，均面阔五间、进深三间，单檐硬山灰瓦顶，砖木结构。拜殿是李氏祭祖的地方，陈列有木刻族规和家训，明间抬梁雕刻精美；殿两端山墙下各有一口水池，左名"廉泉井"，壁书一大"忍"字；右名"让水池"，壁书一大"耐"字，喻示李氏家族的清廉礼让。祖宗殿为宗祠正殿，檐下高悬"魁山堂"匾额。除三大殿外，宗祠内还有族长房、讲礼堂、账房、银库、守卫房等大小房屋69间。讲礼堂是当年李氏族长执行家法和审案的地方，内有一石构件——"过失桥"，长3.81米，宽3.45米，厚0.27米，过去族人若触犯族规需跪在过桥石上，听凭族长训斥与责罚。

宗祠四周均围以城墙，是宗祠的防御堡垒。正面一壁用巨大条石纵联砌成保坎，高约9米；左、右、后三方为依山势逐步升高的石墙堞垛，总长390余米，高6至8米，厚约3米，全部用千斤以上花岗岩条石垒成，随山势蜿蜒。城墙堞垛向外悬挑，依次布设56个射击孔（一称百余枪眼炮孔），严密封锁进祠通道；城墙四角原有炮楼突兀，常年驻乡勇三十余人把守，现已毁。宗祠西南角与东北角山墙各开一门：望华门又名"生门"，门侧建牢房；承恩门又名"死门"，门外一条石板古道直通龙桥天堑。龙桥是一座天然崖桥，横跨龙桥河上，桥头绝壁如削，桥高百丈，桥下水流湍急，轰轰然只闻其声不见其流。穷人"犯法"，判生则从望华门放归；判死则从承恩门绑赴龙桥，在桥上被推下万丈悬崖活活摔死——据不完全统计，新中国成立前仅被李氏族长推下龙桥摔死的贫苦农民就达二十余人，有些不过是抗拒封建婚姻的烈妇。李氏宗祠集政权、军权、族权于一身，是研究鄂西土司社会与封建宗法制度的实物标本。

李氏庄园位于宗祠北面，相距约200米，原为土司黄氏旧宅，李氏占有后在长达百余年的时间里不断扩建，建筑面积6000余平方米，共有24个天井、174间房屋，鳞次栉比，规模宏大。主体建筑三进四厢，坐西北朝东南，砖木并用，中西合璧。庄园最耐人寻味的设计在朝门：朝门建于东北方向，与正屋中轴线成45°夹角——这是讲究堪舆的结果，朝门遥对前山"龙口"（当地河流汇入长江处），取"龙跃大海"之意；门上仿石匾额乳白如玉，上书"青莲美荫"四个大字，铁画银钩，行书洒脱，显然李氏攀附唐代诗仙李白为祖先，以扬其家族身份之不俗。朝门内为规整条石铺成的院坝，宽敞整洁；过院坝上石级十余步入正门，正门后为大厅，一条西式柱廊横贯左右，高大的方柱、弧形柱檐上白灰堆花，气势粗犷而壮丽。中堂左为花厅，圆窗上的蝙蝠图案栩栩如生；中堂右边为账房，天井粉壁上一个一米见方的正楷"忍"字，笔力遒劲。前厅、中堂及后堂地面均以天井隔开，檐下以楼道联结，彩楼迂回，四通八达。庄园两端有小姐楼及绣花楼各一座，飞檐高翘，一东一西遥相呼应。整个庄园内窗棂花样及柱础浮雕多达20余种，依天井为单元，或雕或刻，或圆或方，一院一景，风格既多样又统一。

高仰台李盖五旧宅位于宗祠东北，距庄园约4公里，建于民国三十一年（1942）至三十七年（1948），建筑面积2000余平方米，布局与李氏庄园主体相仿。此地原名"葡萄瓮"，最后一任族长李盖五因兄弟分居造宅于此，嫌原地名俚俗，取"高山仰止"之意更名"高仰台"。1930年（一称1926年），川军贺国强率千余人攻打李氏宗祠，因城池坚固久攻不下，后采用围祠断水的策略方使李氏妥协。事平后，族长李盖五采纳贺国强的建议，扩建围墙将原在祠外的水井包入院内，并在墙外亲书"大水井"三字，每字70厘米见方，行书阴刻，气势狂放——"大水井"之名由此而来，建筑群亦因之得名。大水井古建筑群以土家吊脚楼为主体、以西式柱廊相陪衬，以庙堂建筑为核心、以炮楼城墙相拥护，是巴楚文化、巴汉文化、中外文化交融在建筑上的一个典范。

The Dashuijing (Great Well) Ancient Building Complex sits on the mountainside of Shuijing Village, Baiyangba Town, Lichuan City, 45 km south of Lichuan and about 100 km northeast of Fengjie, Chongqing. Surrounded by mountains — Qiyyue to the north, Hanchi to the south, Jiandao Temple to the east, and Jiulong Pass to the west — the complex consists of three parts: the Li Family Ancestral Hall, Li Liangqing Manor, and the Former Residence of Li Gaiwu at Gaoyangtai, covering over 20,000 square meters with 12,000-plus square meters of buildings. It is the largest and most artistically valuable ancient building complex in southwestern Hubei, hailed as "an architectural epic frozen on the southern bank of the Three Gorges at the foot of the Daba Mountains." It was designated a National Key Cultural Heritage Site in 2001.

Legend has it that the hall and manor originally belonged to the Tujia Huang chieftain as residence and ancestral tomb. In the mid-Qianlong period of the Qing, Li Tinglong and Li Tingfeng moved west from Yuezhou, Hunan; Li Tinglong settled at Dashuijing and Li Tingfeng at Ma'anshan in Yunyang — locally remembered as "the dragon returns to the well, the phoenix roosts on the mountain." Li Tinglong began as a hired hand for the Huang family, farming and distilling liquor; as the Huangs declined, the Lis gradually seized their property and became the greatest landowners on the Hubei-Chongqing border. Over six generations, from the late Ming to 1949 — more than three centuries — they built the hall, manor, and Gaoyangtai residence, each reflecting the architectural style of its era.

The Ancestral Hall, built into the northern slope of Luohan Mountain, facing south to north, was constructed from 1846 to 1849 on the site of the old Huang residence and tomb, with over 3,800 square meters of buildings. It is a palace-style complex with a symmetrical central axis, three rows and three courtyards; the three main halls — Front, Bai (Worship), and Zuzong (Ancestral) — are identical in form, each five bays wide and three deep, with single-eave flush gable roofs of grey tile on brick-wood structure. At the foot of the Bai Hall walls are two pools: "Lianquan Well" with a large character "ren" (forbearance) and "Rangshui Pool" with "nai" (endurance), symbolizing the family's professed integrity. The hall has 69 rooms including the patriarch's quarters, trial hall, accounting room, silver treasury, and guardhouse. The trial hall contains the "Guoshi Bridge" — a stone slab 3.81 m long, 3.45 m wide, 0.27 m thick — upon which offenders knelt for judgment.

The hall is enclosed by defensive walls: a frontal stone rampart about 9 meters high, and side and rear parapets totaling 390-plus meters, 6-8 meters high and 3 meters thick, of granite blocks each weighing over a thousand jin, following the mountain contours. The parapets have 56 (or by some accounts over 100) gun loops commanding all approaches; corner watchtowers (now destroyed) were garrisoned by 30-plus militiamen. Two gates allow entry: Wanghua Gate, the "Gate of Life," beside which stood a prison; and Cheng'en Gate, the "Gate of Death," leading to the Longqiao — a natural cliff bridge over the Longqiao River, with precipices on both banks and the river roaring a hundred zhang below. Those sentenced to death were taken out through the Gate of Death, pushed off the Longqiao, and fell to the abyss — by incomplete count, over 20 poor farmers were executed this way before 1949, some merely women resisting forced marriages.

The Li Manor, 200 meters north of the hall, was expanded over more than a century from the old Huang residence, with 6,000-plus square meters, 24 courtyards, and 174 rooms. The most intriguing design is the portal: set at the northeast at a 45° angle to the main central axis, it faces the "dragon's mouth" where local streams join the Yangtze — a fengshui choice meaning "the dragon leaps to the sea." The portal bears a stone plaque inscribed "Qinglian Meiyin" (Fair Lotus, Fair Shade), a deliberate invocation of the Tang poet Li Bai (whose sobriquet was Qinglian) as the family's ancestor. Inside, a Western-style colonnade runs across the main hall, with tall square columns and floral plasterwork; the manor's windows and column bases display over 20 carving styles, one courtyard one scene. Gaoyangtai, the residence of the last patriarch Li Gaiwu, was built 1942-1948 with over 2,000 square meters, its layout modeled on the main manor. In 1930, the Sichuan warlord He Guoqiang besieged the hall for months without success, then cut off the water supply to force surrender; afterward, Li Gaiwu extended the walls to enclose the well and inscribed the three characters "Da Shui Jing" (Great Well) on the outer wall — hence the complex's name.

五祖寺

Fifth Patriarch Temple (Wuzu Si)

级别：国家级重点文物保护单位 年代：唐至清 地址：黄冈市黄梅县五祖镇东山 类型：宗教建筑·砖木结构

五祖寺原名东山寺，亦名东禅寺，位于黄梅县城东北十二公里的东山之上，大别山主脉东端南沿，与九江隔江相望。唐永徽五年（654年），禅宗五祖弘忍大师跟随四祖道信在双峰山学禅二十余年后，至东山创建此寺，弘扬禅法，时称其禅法为"东山法门"。弘忍被认为是禅宗的实际创始人，门下出神秀、慧能两大弟子，分成南北两宗：北宗神秀一系不久衰落，南宗慧能一系成为禅宗主流，后发展成临济、沩仰、曹洞、云门、法眼五家，合称禅门五宗。五祖寺亦是六祖慧能得衣之地——慧能以一首"菩提本无树，明镜亦非台，本来无一物，何处惹尘埃"的偈语得五祖传法，成为中国佛教史上最著名的公案之一。原中国佛教协会会长赵朴初评价说：中国的禅宗，无不出自黄梅，五祖寺是印度佛教中国化的发源地。

五祖寺在唐代盛极一时，有殿堂楼阁一千余间，僧侣达一千三百余人。唐宣宗敕建并赐额"大中东山寺"，宋真宗改赐"真慧禅寺"，宋英宗御书"天下祖庭"，宋徽宗御书"天下禅林"，元文宗赐额"东山五祖寺"，此名沿用至今。寺屡毁屡建，最后一次毁于清咸丰四年（1854年），仅存十几栋殿堂楼阁。整个佛寺建筑群依山势由上、中、下三部分组成，中轴线平等布局，层次分明，寺院建筑面积近五万平方米。四大主殿——天王殿、大雄宝殿、毗卢殿、真身殿——依山势高低建于中轴线上，亭阁楼台、殿宇僧舍掩映于绿树翠竹之中，重门深通，曲径相连，深得"曲径通幽处，禅房花木深"的意趣。

天王殿始建于唐大中年间，为单檐歇山式建筑，殿门上方刻有宋真宗赐封的"真慧禅寺"匾额，殿前立一对唐代石狮。大雄宝殿原毁于清咸丰四年，1986年按唐代原貌修复，历时七年竣工，建筑面积八百六十五平方米，是中南地区最大的一座大雄宝殿，赵朴初亲笔题写"大雄宝殿"匾额。殿前两侧立北宋崇宁元年（1102年）徽宗御书"天下禅林"及治平年间英宗御书"天下祖庭"碑刻。毗卢殿又名麻城殿，因后来麻城人募捐修建而得名。

真身殿又名祖师殿，是全寺的主体建筑，也是古寺仅存的清代砖木结构宫殿式建筑。原在讲经台下，北宋元祐二年（1087年）移至今址重修。殿前部左为钟亭，右为鼓亭，两亭造型一致相互对称，内与正殿相通，飞檐斗拱，撑角镌有鸟像。正殿正面门上方挂有"真身殿"匾额，门前两旁大柱上塑有金色巨龙，门头横梁雕成空心二龙戏珠，屋顶上有九龙盖顶。正殿后部正中为法雨塔，五祖真身即藏于此，塔壁四周上层有数以百计的石刻小佛像和镌刻匾额。飞虹桥始建于元代，横跨两山涧谷之上，单孔发券，长三十三点六五米，高八点四五米，雄伟壮观，状如飞虹，两端砌有牌坊式门楼。寺内还珍藏北宋治平年间英宗敕镌的"五祖玉印"，黄色玉质，重二点五千克，高十三厘米，印面十一厘米见方，阳刻九叠篆体"天下祖庭五祖大满真身宝印"。寺内现存"大满天下祖庭五祖真身印"汉白玉印一枚和六祖坠腰石一块，上刻"龙塑元年，六祖坠腰石"字样，据说六祖春米时束此石于腰间以增加重量。1983年经国务院批准列为全国第一批重点开放寺庙，2006年被国务院列为第六批全国重点文物保护单位。

The Fifth Patriarch Temple, originally called Dongshan (Eastern Mountain) Temple, is located on Dongshan twelve kilometers northeast of Huangmei County town, on the southern edge of the Dabie Mountains, facing Jiujiang across the Yangtze. In the 5th year of Yonghui, Tang (654), the Fifth Patriarch Hongren, after studying Chan for over twenty years with the Fourth Patriarch Daoxin at Shuangfeng Mountain, came to Dongshan to establish this temple and propagate his teachings, known as the "Dongshan Dharma Gate." Hongren is considered the actual founder of Chan Buddhism; his two principal disciples, Shenxiu and Huineng, founded the Northern and Southern schools respectively. The Southern school under Huineng became the mainstream, later developing into the five houses of Linji, Weiyang, Caodong, Yunmen, and Fayan. This temple is also where the Sixth Patriarch Huineng received the dharma robe after composing the famous verse: "Bodhi has no tree, the mirror has no stand, fundamentally there is not a single thing, where could dust gather?" Zhao Puchu, former president of the Buddhist Association of China, said: "All Chinese Chan Buddhism comes from Huangmei; the Fifth Patriarch Temple is the birthplace of the Sinification of Indian Buddhism."

At its Tang peak, the temple had over one thousand halls and rooms with 1,300 monks. Emperor Xuanzong granted the name "Dazhong Dongshan Temple"; Emperor Zhenzong of Song renamed it "Zhenhui Chan Temple"; Emperor Yingzong inscribed "World Ancestral Court" and Emperor Huizong inscribed "World Chan Forest"; Emperor Wenzong of Yuan granted the name "Dongshan Wuzu Temple," used ever since. The temple was repeatedly destroyed and rebuilt, last destroyed in the 4th year of Xianfeng, Qing (1854), leaving only a dozen-odd halls. The complex rises along the mountain in upper, middle, and lower sections along a central axis, covering nearly 50,000 square meters. The four main halls — Tianwang, Daxiong, Pilu, and Zhenshen — are arranged at increasing elevations, with pavilions and chambers half-hidden among green trees and bamboo.

The Zhenshen Hall, also called the Patriarch Hall, is the temple's main building and the only surviving Qing dynasty brick-wood palace-style structure. Originally below the Sutra-Preaching Platform, it was moved to its current location in the 2nd year of Yuanyou, Northern Song (1087). The front has matching bell and drum pavilions connected to the main hall, with flying eaves, bracket sets, and bird carvings on the supports. The entrance bears the "True Body Hall" plaque, flanked by columns with golden dragons; the lintel is carved with two dragons playing with a pearl, and the roof is crowned with nine dragons. Behind the main hall is the Fayu Pagoda, where Hongren's true body is enshrined, its walls lined with hundreds of small stone Buddha carvings. The Feihong (Flying Rainbow) Bridge, built in the Yuan dynasty, spans the mountain ravine as a single-arch stone bridge 33.65 meters long and 8.45 meters high. The temple preserves the Northern Song Five Patriarch Jade Seal, 2.5 kg, 13 cm tall, with nine-fold seal script reading "World Ancestral Court, Five Patriarch, Great Perfection, True Body Treasure Seal."

荆州城墙

City Wall of Jingzhou

级别：国家级重点文物保护单位 年代：明—清 地址：荆州市荆州区城历史文化旅游区 类型：古城址·砖石结构

荆州古城又名江陵城，是我国保存最为完好的明代府城墙之一，具有延续时间长、跨越朝代多、砖城为主土垣为辅的特点。荆州地处长江中游、江汉平原腹地，是楚文化的发祥地，也是三国文化的繁衍之地；春秋战国时期楚国在城北五公里处的纪南城建都长达四百一十一年，魏蜀吴三国时代这里又是兵家必争的战略要地，一百二十回《三国演义》中有七十二回涉及荆州，"刘备借荆州""关羽大意失荆州"的故事就发生在这块古老的土地上。1996年荆州古城墙被国务院公布为全国重点文物保护单位。

荆州城墙的修造始于两千六百多年前。战国时，楚成王在今城东南隅兴建渚宫，为江陵建置之始；楚幽王在渚宫址上修筑郢都，基地为江边丘陵，故名"江陵"，此后两千年江陵城位置基本不变。东汉及三国时期，蜀将关羽镇守荆州，在渚宫旧址西北筑土城。《水经注》载："（荆州）旧城关羽所筑。某北攻曹仁，吕蒙袭而据之。某曰：此城吾所筑，不可攻也，乃引而退。"关于关羽筑土城，民间有一段"智斗九仙女"的传说：相传关羽镇守荆州，发动十万守城将士挑土堆城，土地神上报天庭，王母娘娘派九仙女下凡欲收回荆州为神地。关羽提出与九仙女比赛筑城，以半夜鸡鸣为限，谁先筑成便拥有管辖权。九仙女仰仗仙法，以衣裙包土兜土成墙，眼看将赢；关羽则用芦苇扎垛堵住缺口，很快筑成，同时使人去鸡笼边拍簸箕学鸡叫，引发全城公鸡提早打鸣。九仙女听闻鸡叫措手不及，黯然退去——这就是荆州城北门外"九女琢"的来历。前来帮助筑城的张飞手拎两筐土赶来，听闻胜讯喜不自胜，将土倒在城边，形成大土丘，后世称"张飞一担土"。南北朝宋代盛宏之《荆州记》对此有生动描绘："一峰回然，西映落月，远而望之，如画扇然。"故"张飞一担土"又名画扇峰，其上曾建六角小亭，被誉为荆州城八景之一。

传说虽不足信，但三国之土城确得到考古印证。1997年10月至1998年3月，考古工作者在荆州城南垣东端（望江楼西侧）坍塌段横向开挖一条长19.2米、宽5米、深9.5米的探沟，通过地层解剖发现：此段暴露在城垣外侧的砖垣建于明初；探沟内土垣自南而北11.3米，为宋代及明初城垣，宋代土垣残高3.75米，明代土垣建在宋代土垣之上残高4.5米；在宋代土垣与明初砖垣之间，有宽0.6至0.8米、高7.3米的宋代碎砖垣遗迹；在探沟中部，宋与明初土城垣之北为五代城垣，其顶部与明初土垣基本取平。由此可见，三国、两晋、五代、宋、明清历代荆州城墙上下重叠，没有发生大的变动——这座城墙是"我国延续时代最长、跨越朝代最多、由土城发展演变而来的唯一古城垣"。东晋时恒温督荆州，将汉东城与关羽所建西城连接起来，其间城垣并未拆除，形成了城中有隔墙的特殊格局。五代后梁时期，南平王高季兴改筑砖城，《五代史》载："季兴大筑重城，复建雄楚楼、望沙楼为扦蔽，执畚锸者十数万人，将校宾友皆负土相助。郭外五十里冢墓多发掘，取砖以甓城。工毕，阴惨之夜，常闻鬼泣，及见磷火焉。"

现存城垣为清顺治三年（1646年）依明城旧基所建，多有明代遗构。整座城呈不规则长方形（一说不规则椭圆形），东西长3.75公里，南北宽1.2公里，城内面积4.5平方公里，城墙周长11.28公里，城墙平均高8.83米，最高处12米，城墙厚约10米，城垛4567个，炮台26座，藏兵洞5座。古城分为三层：外面是水城（护城河），中间是砖城，里面是土城。水城全长约13000米，宽30至250米，平均水深4米，西通太湖，东连长湖，与古运河相连。明代建城时为防止城基下陷、洪水泛城，城墙整体以条石垒砌基脚、青砖砌筑墙体，采用糯米石灰浆灌缝，因而城墙特别坚固。城墙设有瓮城、敌楼、战屋、炮台、藏兵洞、复城门，防御体系完备，历来易守难攻，素有"铁打荆州"之说。

荆州古城原有六座城门，均为双重城门建制，前后城门之间设瓮城，战时可敞开瓮城诱敌深入再出伏兵歼灭。清顺治三年依明代城基重新修筑并重新命名城门：东门曰寅宾门，东南门（小东门）曰公安门，西门曰安澜门，南门曰南纪门，大北门曰拱极门，小北门曰远安门。六座城门之上明清时期均建有城楼，分别是寅宾门的宾阳楼、公安门的楚望楼、南纪门的曲江楼、安澜门的九阳楼、拱极门的朝宗楼和远安门的景龙楼。其中南纪门的曲江楼最享盛名；现拱极门的朝宗楼尚存，建于清道光十八年（1838年），是古城墙上唯一尚存的古建筑；东门的宾阳楼于1987年重建，余下皆毁。上世纪80年代以来，荆州古城又先后增开新东门、新南门和新北门，加上原有的6座城门，荆州古城现有9座城门、城楼2座。除公安门为水门外，其他5座城门外侧都筑有曲城，似半环状将城门围起，曲城前又开一道门，与主城门形成双重城门。

Jingzhou Ancient City, also called Jiangling City, is one of China's best-preserved Ming dynasty prefectural city walls, characterized by its long duration, many dynasties spanned, and the use of brick as the main structure with earthen walls as auxiliary. Jingzhou sits in the middle reaches of the Yangtze, in the heart of the Jianghan Plain — the birthplace of Chu culture and a cradle of Three Kingdoms culture. During the Warring States period, Chu built its capital at Jinan, five kilometers north, for 411 years; in the Three Kingdoms era it was a strategic prize, with 72 of the 120 chapters of "Romance of the Three Kingdoms" involving Jingzhou. The wall was designated a National Key Cultural Heritage Site in 1996.

The wall's construction began over 2,600 years ago. During the Warring States period, King Cheng of Chu built Zhugong Palace in the southeast corner, marking the beginning of Jiangling; King You built Yingdu on the same site, and because the base was a riverside hill, the city was named "Jiangling" — its location unchanged for two thousand years. During the Eastern Han and Three Kingdoms, the Shu general Guan Yu, guarding Jingzhou, built an earthen city northwest of the old Zhugong site. The "Commentary on the Water Classic" records: "The old city was built by Guan Yu. When he attacked Cao Ren to the north, Lü Meng seized it. Guan Yu said: This city was built by me, it cannot be attacked, and withdrew." Folklore tells of Guan Yu outwitting nine fairy maidens sent by the Queen Mother of the West to reclaim Jingzhou: he proposed a wall-building contest, deciding by cockcrow; he tricked the cockerels into crowing early with a winnowing basket, completing his wall of reed bundles first while the fairies fell short. The nine fairies withdrew in shame — the origin of the "Nine Fairies' Mound" north of the city. Zhang Fei, bringing two baskets of earth to help, arrived too late and dumped his load by the eastern gate, forming the hill later called "Zhang Fei's One Load of Earth," also known as "Painted Fan Peak" from the Song dynasty description: "A solitary peak, catching the setting moon in the west, seen from afar like a painted fan."

Though legend, the Three Kingdoms earthen wall is confirmed by archaeology. From October 1997 to March 1998, archaeologists dug a 19.2 m × 5 m × 9.5 m trench at the east end of the south wall and found layered walls from the Three Kingdoms, Jin, Five Dynasties, Song, and Ming — confirming that Jingzhou's wall is "China's longest-continuing, most dynasty-spanning city wall, evolved from earth to brick." The Eastern Jin general Huan Wen connected the old Han eastern city with Guan Yu's western city without demolishing the partition. In the Five Dynasties, Gao Jixing of Later Liang rebuilt in brick, mobilizing tens of thousands of laborers who, according to the "History of the Five Dynasties," even excavated tombs within fifty li for bricks.

The surviving wall was built in the 3rd year of Shunzhi, Qing (1646) on Ming foundations, with many Ming remnants. The city is an irregular rectangle (some say oval), 3.75 km east-west, 1.2 km north-south, 4.5 square kilometers, 11.28 km in circumference, averaging 8.83 meters high (maximum 12), about 10 meters thick, with 4,567 crenellations, 26 gun platforms, and 5 soldier-hiding caves. The city has three layers: the outer water city (moat), the middle brick wall, and the inner earth wall. The moat runs about 13,000 meters, 30-250 meters wide, 4 meters deep. Ming builders used glutinous rice-lime mortar between stone footings and brick facing, making the wall extraordinarily solid. Complete with barbicans, enemy towers, gun platforms, and hidden soldier caves, it earned the name "Iron-Forged Jingzhou."

The city originally had six double-gate entries with barbicans between front and rear gates, designed to lure enemies in and ambush them. In 1646 the gates were renamed: Yinbin (East), Gong'an (Southeast), Anlan (West), Nanji (South), Gongji (Great North), and Yuan'an (Lesser North). Each gate had a tower — Binyang, Chuwang, Qujiang, Jiuyang, Chaozong, and Jinglong respectively. Of these, the Qujiang Tower was most celebrated; the Chaozong Tower at Gongji Gate, built in 1838, is the only surviving original gate tower; Binyang Tower was rebuilt in 1987. Since the 1980s three new gates have been added, bringing the total to nine gates and two surviving towers.

襄阳古隆中

Ancient Longzhong in Xiangyang

级别：国家级重点文物保护单位 年代：明、清 地址：襄阳市襄城区隆中路6号 类型：纪念建筑

古隆中位于襄阳市襄城区城西十三公里处，是一处群山环抱的小小山谷。南朝梁陈时期顾野王《舆地志》记载："隆中者，空中也。行其上空空然有声。"隆中因此而得名。如罗贯中所描绘，其环境"山不高而秀雅；水不深而澄清；地不广而平坦；林不大而茂密"——青山绿水环绕，山谷中有良田数顷，山腰有古井清泉，是处既可潜心读书又能躬耕陇亩的地方。从大环境来看，襄阳据南北驿道的要冲，隆中虽地属山林却并不闭塞。此处即相传一代名相诸葛亮出山之前躬耕苦读的隐居之所，著名的"三顾茅庐"和"隆中对"的历史故事就发生于此——东汉建安十二年（207年），刘备为实现兴汉大业四处访求贤士，到隆中"三顾茅庐"，与诸葛亮共商大计；诸葛亮全面分析形势，提出著名的《隆中对》，奠定了"三分天下"的基础。1996年古隆中被国务院列为全国重点文物保护单位。

诸葛亮出山后，此处空留草屋若干间，曾易为董姓所居，后随董家没落而衰。晋代有碑铭记述，唐代建有武侯庙，此后多有损毁、重建。至明中叶弘治年间，襄简王朱见淑沼其宅址建陵，并于故宅东侧山梁另建小祠以奉祀武侯，逐渐形成以三顾堂为中心的"隆中十景"，后扩展为十二景。明嘉靖、万历和清康熙年间又多次重修，近代当代也多有维修和新建。现存建筑多为清代所建，依山势错落、散布于约2平方公里的隆中山南麓，大都以诸葛亮当年的遗址而定名，主要建筑和胜迹有"古隆中"牌坊、抱膝亭、武侯祠、三顾堂、草庐亭、野云庵（卧龙深处）、襄王陵、躬耕田、小虹桥、梁父岩、老龙洞、半月溪等，山中遍存石碑48通。

石牌坊是隆中的标志性建筑，建于清光绪十九年，为仿木结构四柱三门楼式，青石组装而成，高约6米，宽约10米。牌坊正中的字碑上雕刻"古隆中"三个大字，两边大书"澹泊明志""宁静致远"的名句——诸葛亮《诫子书》中"非澹泊无以明志，非宁静无以致远"的箴言，被刻在纪念他的牌坊上，恰如其分。武侯祠始建于晋朝，是祀奉诸葛亮的祠宇，位于隆中山腰，后历经隋、唐、宋、元、明、清、民国，兴废频仍；现存建筑为清康熙三十八年（1699年）荆襄观察使蒋兴岂重建，为四进三院层台式建筑，主殿内有诸葛亮及其子孙诸葛瞻、诸葛尚塑像供人瞻仰、祭拜，祠内还有一株四百余年的金桂，颇有灵气。三顾堂重建于清康熙五十八年（1719年），为纪念刘备三顾草庐而修建，院落外围碑廊的墙壁上嵌着明清时期的碑刻36通，其中最具代表性的是《隆中对》、前《出师表》、后《出师表》等碑刻。

抱膝亭建于清光绪十四年至十九年（1888—1893年），由湖北提督程文炳在郧襄观察使赵宏恩所修建的"躬耕亭"位置上重修。相传此处原有一块大石头，诸葛亮寓居隆中时，在闲暇之余会坐在这块大石头上吟唱山东老家的小调《梁父吟》，一方面抒发思乡之情，一方面抒发远大抱负。整座亭阁在绿茵之中，六角形，三檐三层，檐角高翘，造型挺秀端庄。草庐亭是诸葛亮当年在隆中卧读躬耕、生活起居之处，电视连续剧《诸葛亮》曾在此拍摄。武侯祠大门上方刻着"汉诸葛丞相武侯祠"，下面代表"福、禄、寿"的三个小人，两边还有"掀天、揭地"四个大字，寓意着诸葛亮出山后辅佐刘备，使东汉末年群雄割据的混乱局面发生了翻天覆地的变化，并最终形成了三足鼎立之势。

Ancient Longzhong is located thirteen kilometers west of Xiangcheng District, Xiangyang City, in a small valley surrounded by mountains. The "Yudi Zhi" (Geographical Records) of the Liang-Chen period records: "Longzhong means 'empty in the air'; walking above it, one hears a hollow sound" — hence the name. As Luo Guanzhong depicted, its environment is "the mountains are not high but elegant; the water is not deep but clear; the land is not broad but flat; the forest is not large but dense" — green hills and clear water encircling a valley with fields and springs, a place fit for both study and farming. From the broader setting, Xiangyang commands the strategic point of the north-south post road; though Longzhong lies in mountain forests, it is not isolated. This is the legendary hermitage where Zhuge Liang, a generation's great prime minister, farmed and studied before entering the world; the famous stories of "Three Visits to the Thatched Cottage" and the "Longzhong Dialogue" took place here — in the 12th year of Jian'an, Eastern Han (207), Liu Bei came thrice to seek the scholar, and Zhuge Liang presented the celebrated "Longzhong Plan" that laid the foundation for the tripartite division of the realm. Longzhong was designated a National Key Cultural Heritage Site in 1996.

After Zhuge Liang left, only several thatched cottages remained; they were briefly occupied by the Dong family and declined with them. The Jin dynasty left stele records, and a Wuhou Temple was built in the Tang; thereafter the site was repeatedly damaged and rebuilt. In the mid-Ming Hongzhi reign, Prince Xiang Jian Zhu Jianshu built his mausoleum on the cottage site and erected a small shrine on the eastern ridge to honor Wuhou, gradually forming the "Ten Scenes of Longzhong" centered on the Sangu Hall, later expanded to twelve. The Kangxi reign and modern times saw further restorations. Surviving buildings, mostly Qing, are scattered over about 2 square kilometers on the southern slope of Longzhong Mountain, mostly named after Zhuge Liang's original sites: the "Ancient Longzhong" stone archway, Baoxi Pavilion, Wuhou Shrine, Sangu Hall, Caolu Pavilion, Yeyun Hermitage, Prince Xiang's Mausoleum, Gongtian (Farming) Field, Xiaohong Bridge, Liangfu Rock, Laolong Cave, and Banyue Stream, with 48 stone steles throughout the mountain.

The stone archway is Longzhong's landmark, built in the 19th year of Guangxu, Qing, an imitation-wood four-column three-gate structure of blue stone, about 6 meters high and 10 meters wide. The central tablet bears "Ancient Longzhong," flanked by the maxims "Tranquility reveals aspiration" and "Stillness reaches far" — from Zhuge Liang's "Admonition to My Son": "Without tranquility, no aspiration can be revealed; without stillness, nothing can be reached far." The Wuhou Shrine, first built in the Jin, stands on the mountainside; the surviving structure was rebuilt in the 38th year of Kangxi, Qing (1699), by the Jing-Xiang Intendant Jiang Xingqi — a four-courtyard, three-tier terrace building housing statues of Zhuge Liang and his descendants Zhuge Zhan and Zhuge Shang, with a 400-year-old golden osmanthus tree. The Sangu Hall, rebuilt in the 58th year of Kangxi (1719), commemorates Liu Bei's three visits; its outer corridor walls embed 36 Ming-Qing steles, most notably the "Longzhong Plan," the "Former Memorial," and the "Later Memorial."

The Baoxi (Embracing-Knee) Pavilion, built 1888-1893 by the Hubei commander Cheng Wenbing on the site of the old "Farming Pavilion," marks the spot where, according to legend, Zhuge Liang would sit on a great stone in his leisure, singing the "Liangfu Tune" of his Shandong homeland — partly to express homesickness, partly to voice his far-reaching ambitions. The hexagonal, three-eave, three-tier pavilion stands amid greenery, its corners upturned, graceful and dignified. The Caolu (Thatched Cottage) Pavilion marks where Zhuge Liang lived and farmed; the TV drama "Zhuge Liang" was filmed here. Above the Wuhou Shrine gate is inscribed "Han Chancellor Zhuge Wuhou Shrine," beneath it three small figures for "fortune, prosperity, longevity," and on either side the four characters "Heaven-lifting, Earth-turning" — meaning that after Zhuge Liang's emergence, the warlord chaos of the late Eastern Han was overturned and a tripartite balance ultimately formed.

武当山古建筑群

Ancient Building Complex in the Wudang Mountains

级别：世界文化遗产·国家级重点文物保护单位 年代：唐、明、清 地址：十堰市丹江口市武当山 类型：宗教建筑·砖木、铜铸、石结构

武当山这座方圆八百里的名山，位于十堰丹江口市境内，东临襄阳，北临丹江口水库，又名太和山、玄岳山、仙室山。自唐代始建五龙祠开始，武当山一直是道教的重要福地。宋元以来，先后开五龙观为五龙宫，又建紫霄宫和南岩天乙真庆宫，武当山逐渐形成了以真武崇拜为主要特征的武当道教。根据道教经典记载，真武本为净乐国太子，入武当山修道，历四十二年而功成飞升，被玉帝封为北方之神和水神，武当山也因此得名——"武当"即"非真武不足以当之"之意。明初，著名道士张三丰曾隐居此山，当时武当山宫观悉毁于兵火，张三丰对人言："此山异日必大兴。"果然到了燕王朱棣以北方藩王起兵夺位之后，此语应验。

明成祖朱棣以"靖难"之役从侄儿建文帝手中夺取皇位后，声称其得天下曾得真武神相助，于是以"奉天靖难"为名，在真武道场武当山大兴土木。永乐十年（1412年），朱棣遣隆平侯张信等率军夫工匠三十多万人，以十二年功夫在武当山上建成八宫二观、三十六庵堂、七十二岩庙，此外还修建了三十九座桥、十二座亭子以及从山麓到金顶的七十余公里登山神道。当时的武当山拥有全国最宏伟的宫观建筑、最庞大的教团组织，被誉为"天下第一名山"。由于施工中征集了全国各地的工匠，各种建筑手法在此得以汇集；特别是在建筑选址上，指派著名的风水家进行地形勘测，使这些建筑物在体量和规模上与环境高度协调，体现了道教玄妙神奇的风格。1994年12月，武当山古建筑群被列入《世界遗产名录》；2006年被国务院公布为第六批全国重点文物保护单位。

武当山建筑群中现存建筑有太和宫、南岩宫、紫霄宫、遇真宫四座宫殿，玉虚宫、五龙宫二处遗址，以及元和观、复真观和大量庵堂、神祠、岩庙等，共有古建筑二百余栋，建筑面积五万平方米，占地面积一百多万平方米。众多的建筑中，用材广泛，有木构、铜铸、石雕等，都达到了极高的技艺水平。其中最负盛名的是金殿、紫霄宫、南岩宫和治世玄岳牌坊。

金殿位于武当山主峰天柱峰顶端一个面积约一百六十平方米的石筑平台正中，是武当山古建筑群的最高点和核心。金殿建于明永乐十四年（1416年），是一座明代铜铸鎏金仿木结构宫殿式建筑，朝向东偏南八度。殿面阔三间四点四米，进深三间三点一五米，高五点五四米；四周立柱十二根，柱上叠架额、枋及重翘重昂与单翘重昂斗拱，分别承托上下檐部，构成重檐庑殿式屋顶。正脊两端铸龙对峙，四壁于立柱之间装四抹头格扇门。殿内顶部作平棋天花，铸浅雕流云纹样，线条柔和流畅；地面以紫色石纹墁地，洗磨光洁。殿内于后壁屏风前设神坛，塑真武大帝坐像，左侍金童捧册，右侍玉女端宝，水火二将执旗捧剑拱卫两厢；坛下玄武一尊，为龟蛇合体。神坛上方高悬鎏金匾额，上铸清圣祖康熙手迹"金光妙相"四字。殿外檐际悬盘龙斗边鎏金牌额，上竖铸"金殿"二字。殿体各部分件采用失蜡法铸造，遍体鎏金，无论瓦作、木作构件，结构严谨，合缝精密——其余全部构件在北京用铜铸成后，经运河转长江、汉水运至天柱峰组装而成，总重量约九十吨，真武大帝铜像一尊重达万斤。虽历五百多年风雨雷电、酷暑严寒，至今仍金灿绚烂，辉煌如初，是中国古代建筑和铸造工艺中的一颗璀璨明珠。围绕天柱峰还建有紫禁城，建于永乐十七年（1419年），延天柱峰环绕，周长三百四十五米，墙基厚二点四米，墙厚一点八米，城墙最高处达十米，用条石依岩砌筑，每块条石重达五百多千克，按中国天堂的模式建有东、南、西、北四座石雕仿木结构的城楼象征天门。

紫霄宫是武当山现存宫观中规模最大、保存最完整的一座。宋宣和年间（1119—1125年）始建，元代重建名"紫霄元圣宫"，明永乐十年（1412年）扩建，嘉靖三十一年（1552年）扩建到八百零六间。紫霄宫占地面积七点四万平方米，现存庙宇一百八十二间，建筑面积八千五百五十三平方米。主殿紫霄殿高十八米，宽三十米，进深十二米，是武当山保存下的唯一的一座重檐歇山式木结构殿堂，其结构、布局科学合理，艺术风格协调统一，同自然环境融为一体。殿内神龛中供奉明代御制铜铸鎏金真武神像，为真武老、中、青年龄和文、武装束的塑像；殿中还有一对"铁树开花"灯，上饰龙凤、孔雀、花叶，为国内仅有，已被定为国宝。

南岩宫始建于元至元二十二年（1285年）至元至大三年（1310年），明永乐十年（1412年）扩建，位于独阳岩下，山势飞翥，状如垂天之翼，以峰峦秀美而著名。现存建筑二十一栋，建筑面积三千五百零五平方米，占地九万平方米。主体建筑天乙真庆宫石殿，建于元至大三年（1310年）以前，面阔十一米，进深六点六米，通高六点八米，梁、柱、门、窗等均以青石雕凿而成——这是中国现存最大的石雕仿木结构殿堂。殿前伸出崖外的龙头香，长三米，宽仅零点三三米，横空挑出，下临深谷，龙头上置一小香炉，状极峻险，是武当山最著名的奇观之一。

治世玄岳牌坊建于明嘉靖三十一年（1552年），位于武当山镇东四公里处，为进入武当山的第一道门户，又名玄岳门。坊为石凿仿大木建筑结构，三间四柱五楼，高十一点九米，阔十四点五米。坊柱高六点四米，柱周设夹杆石以铁箍加固。此坊结构简练，构件富于变化，全用卯榫拼合，装配均衡严谨；坊身装饰华丽，雕刻精工，运用线刻、圆雕、浮雕等方法，雕刻了人物、动物和花卉图案等，是南方石作牌楼之佳作，也是明代石雕艺术珍品。明间横式牌匾上刻嘉靖皇帝赐额"治世玄岳"四字。此外，复真观（又名太子坡）的五云楼高十五点八米，是武当山最高的木构建筑；其最顶层的"一柱十二梁"——一根主体立柱上穿凿十二根梁枋，交叉叠搁，计算周密，是中国古建筑中的孤例。

Mount Wudang, a famous mountain of eight hundred li in circumference, lies in Danjiangkou, Shiyan, Hubei, adjoining Xiangyang to the east and the Danjiangkou Reservoir to the north. Also called Taihe Mountain or Xuanwu Mountain, it has been an important Daoist sanctuary since the Tang dynasty, when the Five Dragon Shrine was first built. Through Song and Yuan times, Five Dragon Palace, Zixiao Palace, and Nanyan Zhenqing Palace were successively constructed, and Wudang Daoism with its Zhenwu worship took shape. According to Daoist scriptures, Zhenwu was originally a prince of JingLe Kingdom who cultivated Dao on Wudang for forty-two years before ascending to immortality, and was appointed by the Jade Emperor as the god of the north and of water — hence the mountain's name, meaning "only Zhenwu is worthy of this place." The legendary Daoist Zhang Sanfeng once dwelt here in the early Ming and prophesied: "This mountain will one day greatly flourish."

After the Yongle Emperor Zhu Di seized the throne from his nephew the Jianwen Emperor through the "Jingnan Campaign," he claimed that Zhenwu had aided his victory, and on the pretext of "Heaven-ordained Pacification" he launched the largest construction campaign in Wudang's history. In the 10th year of Yongle (1412), Zhu Di dispatched the Marquis of Longping Zhang Xin to lead over 300,000 soldiers and artisans in a twelve-year effort that produced eight palaces, two temples, thirty-six hermitages, and seventy-two cliff shrines, plus thirty-nine bridges, twelve pavilions, and a pilgrimage path of over seventy kilometers from the foot to the Golden Summit. With craftsmen gathered from across the empire and site selection supervised by eminent fengshui masters, the complex achieved remarkable harmony with its environment. In December 1994 the Ancient Building Complex was inscribed on the World Heritage List; in 2006 it was designated a National Key Cultural Heritage Site.

The surviving complex comprises four palaces — Taihe, Nanyan, Zixiao, and Yuzhen — two ruin sites — Yuxu and Wulong — plus Yuanhe Temple, Fuzhen Temple, and numerous hermitages and cliff shrines, totaling over two hundred ancient buildings, 50,000 square meters of floor area, on more than one million square meters of land. Among them, the Golden Hall, Zixiao Palace, Nanyan Palace, and the Zhishi Xuanyue Archway are the most renowned.

The Golden Hall stands atop Tianzhu Peak, the highest point of Wudang, centered on a stone platform of about 160 square meters. Built in the 14th year of Yongle, Ming (1416), it is a bronze-cast, gold-gilded, wood-imitation palace-style structure, oriented eight degrees east of south. The hall is three bays wide (4.4 m), three bays deep (3.15 m), and 5.54 m high, with twelve perimeter columns supporting double-eave hipped roofs; the main ridge bears paired dragons at either end. All components were cast in Beijing by the lost-wax method, gold-gilded throughout, then transported via the Grand Canal, Yangtze, and Han River to Tianzhu Peak for assembly, totaling about 90 tons, with the Zhenwu statue alone weighing ten thousand catties. After more than five centuries of wind, rain, lightning, and extreme temperatures, it still gleams as new — a crowning achievement of Chinese casting and architecture. Around the peak runs the Purple Forbidden City, built in the 17th year of Yongle (1419), 345 meters in circumference, its walls of stone blocks each weighing over 500 kg, with four gate-towers symbolizing the gates of Heaven.

Zixiao Palace, the largest and best-preserved surviving temple on Wudang, was first built in the Xuanhe period of Song (1119-1125), rebuilt in Yuan as "Zixiao Yuan Sheng Palace," and expanded by Yongle in 1412 and again by Jiajing in 1552 to 806 rooms. Its main hall, Zixiao Dian, 18 m high, 30 m wide, and 12 m deep, is Wudang's only surviving double-eave flush-hipped wooden hall; its imperial bronze-gilded Zhenwu statues and the unique "iron tree in bloom" lamp — a national treasure — are among its treasures. Nanyan Palace, built 1285-1310 in Yuan and expanded by Yongle in 1412, features the Tianyi Zhenqing Stone Hall, China's largest stone-carved wood-imitation structure (6.8 m high), and the famed "Dragon Head Incense" — a three-meter-long altar projecting over the abyss with a censer at its tip, breathtakingly perilous. The Zhishi Xuanyue Archway, built in the 31st year of Jiajing (1552) as Wudang's first gateway, is a four-column, five-roof stone archway 11.9 m high and 14.5 m wide, bearing the Jiajing Emperor's inscription "Governing the World through the Mysterious Mount Yue" — a masterpiece of Ming stone carving. In Fuzhen Temple (Prince's Slope), the Five Cloud Tower, 15.8 m high — Wudang's tallest wooden building — features the unique "One Pillar Twelve Beams" construction at its topmost story.

明显陵

Xianling Tomb of the Ming Dynasty

级别：世界文化遗产·国家级重点文物保护单位 年代：明正德十四年至嘉靖三十八年（1519—1559） 地址：荆门市钟祥市城东北7.5公里纯德山 类型：礼制建筑·陵墓

明显陵是明世宗嘉靖皇帝朱厚熜的生身父亲兴献王朱祐杬和王妃蒋氏的合葬墓，是中国历史上一座独具特色的帝王陵，也是世界文化遗产"明清皇家陵寝"的重要组成部分。朱祐杬是明宪宗朱见深的第四子，成化十二年（1476年）生，成化二十三年（1487年）封为兴王，弘治七年（1494年）就藩湖广安陆州（今钟祥市），正德十四年（1519年）薨逝，明武宗朱厚照赐谥为"献"，在松林山择地按亲王规制营建坟园。明正德十六年（1521年），明武宗朱厚照无嗣崩殂，据太祖朱元璋"兄终弟及"的遗训，袭封为兴王不久的朱厚熜被迎往北京继承皇位，改年号为嘉靖，即明世宗。

嘉靖皇帝登基后的第一件事就是追封自己的父亲为皇帝，但大臣们认为新帝应追认他的叔叔孝宗为父皇，并称他的亲生父亲为"皇叔"。为此君臣双方各执一词，争论激烈，随之又产生一系列关于皇位正统和皇室利益的争执，直到嘉靖皇帝执政的中期，世宗统治彻底稳固，这些争执才平息下来。史学家称这件事为"大礼仪"事件，它对整个明代中叶的社会思想和信仰都有着重大的影响，也在很大程度上决定了后来的北京城的面貌。嘉靖三年（1524年），世宗为生父母上尊号"皇考恭睿献皇帝""章圣慈仁皇太后"，改称孝宗为"皇伯考"，生父为"皇考"——皇考之争是"大礼仪"事件中嘉靖皇帝取得的首个胜利，而显陵的改建便是这次胜利的战利品。显陵始建于明正德十四年（1519年），最初按照藩王陵寝的规格修造；嘉靖三年以帝陵规制改建，至嘉靖三十八年（1559年）最终建成，历时四十年，是中国中南地区唯一的明代帝陵和最大的明代单体帝王陵。

陵区总面积约一百八十三公顷，其中陵寝部分占地五十二公顷，有外罗城、内罗城两层城墙环绕。外罗城高三米（一说六点五米），厚一点六米（一说一点九米），周长三千六百米（一说四千七百三十米），红墙黄瓦，金碧辉煌，蜿蜒起伏于山峦叠嶂之中，轮廓似一个细长的宝瓶。陵区后部的自然山丘为祖山，作为陵寝的依托；两侧的山体作为环护，中间台地安排建筑，九曲河蜿蜒其间，前面山丘为屏山，构成前朱雀、后玄武、左青龙、右白虎的风水格局，使建筑与环境和谐统一，"陵制与山水相称"。

外罗城最南端为双重陵门——新红门与旧红门，两道门都是砖石砌筑的无梁殿，单檐歇山琉璃屋顶。旧红门建于嘉靖三年（1524年），是中轴线的正门；新红门建于嘉靖十八年（1539年），是显陵的第一道门户。新红门从地形走向完全可以直接建在神路中轴线上，但它却偏离中轴线，东靠纯德山，西临外明塘——这主要出于风水的原因：嘉靖帝回钟祥时带了风水大师廖文政，他察看显陵地脉后说，显陵中轴偏向了西南，若略偏东南则更佳。嘉靖皇帝采纳其建议，加建新红门及外罗城，延深了龙形神道，将新红门以龙尾的形式摆向东方，使整个龙形神道更像一条活龙、一条游动的龙。

旧红门内是一条神形兼具如游龙般的龙形神道，走向不取笔直形态而有意呈"曲路"，过棂星门后有一条长达二百九十米的弯曲神道至第五座石桥。神道正中矗立着高大的睿功圣德碑亭，平面布局为方形，占地三百三十四平方米，汉白玉台基，下设石须弥座，上为重檐歇山顶，四边各开有券门，正中立龙首龟趺睿功圣德碑。碑亭后六十三米处设御桥，过桥便是陵区最主要的墓饰建筑：迎面为通高十二米的汉白玉望柱，下为方形须弥座，柱身为六棱形，二层束腰云盘托着圆柱形有云龙纹浮雕的望柱头。望柱后神道左右两侧分布着石像生群，计有狮子、獬豸、卧骆驼、卧象、麒麟、立马、卧马各一对，文臣、武将各二对。棂星门作为石像生的依托，以庑殿式影壁夹着三座两柱冲天式石牌坊组成，六根方柱柱顶各置独角神兽一只，额枋上雕刻云纹和火焰宝珠。

过九曲御河最后一道御桥便是内明塘，内明塘之后便是显陵的陵宫区。九曲御河的设计与风水术有密切关系——《葬经》云："风水之法，得水为上，藏风次之。"弯曲的河道与中轴神路上的五座石拱桥左右横穿而过，将陵区地面划分为几个不同的区域，恰到好处地发挥了山、水、树木的空间限定作用。内外明塘的建置为其他明代帝陵所没有——"明塘"取"明堂"谐音，为聚"气"之所，均处于风水术中"明塘"的方位，是风水格局中有关"生气"的关键部位。内明塘位于祾恩门前，是人工开凿的圆形池塘，靠近核心"穴"的位置，有集聚地下水的功能；外明塘是根据原有天然池塘改建，正好位于陵区的前端，对纯德山的地气有界护的作用。

内罗城陵宫区建有祾恩门、祾恩大殿、明楼、茔城（宝城）、瑶台等，其中呈"八"字形结构的是两大茔城（即前、后宝城），瑶台连接着两座地下玄宫——这是中国历代帝陵中唯一的"一陵两冢"建制。前宝城呈椭圆形，东西一百一十二米，南北一百二十五米，正德十五年（1519年）以藩王规制所建；后宝城呈圆形，直径一百一十米，嘉靖十八年（1539年）以帝规制所建——两座宝城之间以瑶台相连，瑶台为长方形，面阔十一点五米，进深四十点五米。显陵之所以建有两座宝城，一是因为打开地宫时发现渗水，嘉靖皇帝决定增建地宫（即后宝城）；二是为了妥善安葬父母——前地宫始建于正德十四年（1519年），是兴献王朱祐杬死后按藩王规制所建；后地宫建于嘉靖十八年（1539年），是嘉靖南巡到钟祥时为父母亲钦定的新玄宫及后宝城。两座宝城之间以瑶台相连，构成一个相互关联的整体，形成了"一陵两冢"的特殊格局——前后两座地宫相差二十年修建。城台前有天然巨石刻成的五供台和两根望柱，柱顶各置一只圣兽用来镇邪。1988年显陵被国务院公布为第三批全国重点文物保护单位，2000年11月30日被联合国教科文组织列入《世界遗产名录》。

— 英文 English —

Xianling is the joint burial tomb of Zhu Youyuan, Prince Xian of the Ming, and his consort Lady Jiang — the father and mother of the Jiajing Emperor Zhu Houcong. A uniquely distinctive imperial mausoleum and a key component of the World Heritage "Imperial Tombs of the Ming and Qing Dynasties," it stands on Mount Chunde, 7.5 km northeast of Zhongxiang, Jingmen. Zhu Youyuan, fourth son of Emperor Xianzong, was born in 1476, enfeoffed as Prince Xing in 1487, sent to his appanage in Anlu (present-day Zhongxiang) in 1494, and died in 1519, receiving the posthumous name "Xian." In 1521, Emperor Wuzong died without issue, and following the founding Emperor Zhu Yuanzhang's principle of "brother succeeding brother," the newly enfeoffed Prince Xing, Zhu Houcong, was brought to Beijing to inherit the throne as the Jiajing Emperor.

The Jiajing Emperor's first act was to posthumously elevate his father to imperial status, but the court insisted he should instead adopt his uncle Emperor Xiaozong as "Imperial Father" and demote his own father to "Imperial Uncle." The resulting "Great Rites Controversy" dragged on for years, shaping mid-Ming politics, religion, and the very layout of Beijing. In 1524, the emperor prevailed: his parents received the titles "Imperial Father Gongrui Xian Emperor" and "Empress Zhangsheng Ciren," and the upgrading of Xianling from princely to imperial tomb was the spoils of that victory. Originally built in 1519 to princely specifications, Xianling was rebuilt to imperial standards from 1524 and completed in 1559 after forty years — the only Ming imperial tomb in central China and the largest single Ming imperial mausoleum.

The mausoleum covers about 183 hectares, of which 52 are the tomb precinct, enclosed by double walls — the outer wall of red and yellow tile, about 3,600 meters long, winding over the hills like a slender treasure bottle. The site follows the fengshui pattern of " Vermilion Bird before, Black Tortoise behind, Azure Dragon on the left, White Tiger on the right," with natural hills as ancestral mountain, flanking ranges, a central terrace for buildings, and the Nine-Bend River meandering through.

The southernmost gate is the New Red Gate (built 1539), deliberately off the central axis — on the advice of the fengshui master Liao Wenzheng, who judged the axis too far southwest — so that the "Dragon-shaped Spirit Path" curves like a living dragon, its tail swinging eastward. The Old Red Gate (built 1524) is the axial main gate. Along the spirit path stand the square Ruigong Shengde Stele Pavilion (334 sq m, double-eave flush-hipped roof), a 12-meter white marble watch pillar, twelve pairs of stone figures (lions, xiezhi, camels, elephants, qilin, horses, civil and military officials), and the Lingxing Gate — three two-pillar stone archways between hip-roofed screen walls, each pillar topped by a single-horned divine beast.

Beyond the last bridge over the Nine-Bend River lies the Inner Bright Pond (Nei Mingtang), and beyond it the tomb precinct with its Lien Gate, Lien Hall, Ming Tower, and twin Treasure Cities. The "One Tomb, Two Burials" layout is unique among Chinese imperial tombs: the elliptical Front Treasure City (112 m × 125 m, built 1519 to princely rank) and the circular Rear Treasure City (110 m diameter, built 1539 to imperial rank) are linked by the rectangular Yaotai platform (11.5 × 40.5 m). The dual structure arose because the original crypt was found to seep water, prompting the emperor to build a new one; and because he wished to bury both parents properly — the front crypt for Prince Xian, the rear for the joint burial of emperor and empress. In 1988 Xianling was designated a National Key Cultural Heritage Site, and in 2000 it was inscribed on the World Heritage List.

东坡赤壁

Dongpo Red Cliff

级别：国家级重点文物保护单位 年代：唐、宋、清 地址：黄冈市黄州区公园路 类型：风景建筑·砖木结构

出黄冈古城西之汉川门，北面有一山陡峭如壁，山石颜色赤红，两汉时便有名为赤壁，因其山色赭赤、陡峭如壁、状若悬鼻而又名赤鼻。西晋初年，龙骧将军蒯思为了纪念赤壁之战，始在山上建横江馆；唐代诗人杜牧、宋初散文家王禹偁先后被贬谪黄冈，赤壁之名日盛。北宋元丰三年（1080年），苏轼因"乌台诗案"贬谪黄州。在黄州四年多的时间里，苏轼处逆境而志存高远，遭挫折却大度豁达，每有闲情，常与友人泛舟赤壁下，赏明月、临清风、穿激流、荡碧波，抒怀壮志，写下了旷世奇作——前后《赤壁赋》和《念奴娇·赤壁怀古》。"大江东去，浪淘尽，千古风流人物"——二赋一词灿若星斗，遂使黄州赤壁天下闻名。在苏子之前，黄州本是边远蛮荒之地；而在苏子之后，此地文风鼎盛，成为历代文人墨客的"朝圣"之所。清康熙末年，黄州知府郭朝祚将"黄州赤壁"更名为"东坡赤壁"并沿用至今。

南宋末年，赤壁毁于兵燹，元、明、清三代多有修葺，现存建筑大多系清同治七年（1868年）重修。赤壁矶东高西低，主要的建筑随山势而建，嵌于峭壁石矶之上，或临岩据险，或依山连岸，曲曲含方，小巧别致，以小径、院落相连，与地形结合浑然一体。主要的建筑物有二堂、三楼、二阁、一斋、九亭，又有一峰、一像，建筑与园林布局兼采南方园林之秀丽与官式风格之典雅庄重，亭台楼阁取名含蓄，温文尔雅，具有浓厚的人文色彩。

二赋堂是东坡赤壁最著名的建筑，始建于清初，同治七年（1868年）重建，因纪念苏轼赤壁二赋而得名。堂内正中木壁高约两丈，正面刻有前《赤壁赋》，楷书，由清人程之桢书写；背面刻有后《赤壁赋》，汉隶魏碑二体相兼，由近代著名书法家李开侁书写。两幅木刻每字直径三寸有余，前者豪迈俊逸，后者苍劲有力。"二赋堂"匾额为清人李鸿章亲笔题写。堂前对联为辛亥革命领袖黄兴所撰："才子重文章，凭他二赋八诗，都争传苏东坡两游赤壁；英雄造时势，待我三年五载，必艳说湖南客小住黄州。"堂内还嵌有民国大总统徐世昌（号水竹村人）书写的二赋与楹联："古今往事千帆去，风月秋怀一篴知。"——亦为世人所重。

坡仙亭坐落在赤壁矶头，是东坡赤壁最重要的亭阁之一。亭内三面壁上嵌有历代名人的书画碑刻二十六块，其中苏轼的书画碑刻十四块最为世人注重——东坡先生著名的草书《念奴娇·赤壁怀古》即刻于此亭中。"坡仙"之称来自南宋诗人戴复古的诗句："白鸟沧波上，黄州赤壁边。长江酹明月，更忆老坡仙。"酹江亭始建于宋代，原名酹月亭，取苏轼《念奴娇·赤壁怀古》词"一樽还酹江月"之意；亭内嵌有历代名人的书画碑刻十四块，其中以康熙御书《赤壁赋》与清人叶志诜"一笔寿"最为知名——此亭亦为清同治七年重修。

留仙阁于清代光绪十年（1884年）修建，落成之日恰逢十二月十九日苏轼生日，阁内原塑苏轼坐像，取坡仙长留阁内之意，名之"留仙阁"；阁中嵌有历代名人书画碑刻十二块。栖霞楼系宋代黄州四大名楼之一，位于赤壁最高处，有"江淮绝胜"之誉；因楼背山面江，落日时晚霞染红大江，照映楼身，如霞归栖，故名栖霞楼——今楼名系茅盾亲题。挹爽楼上下两层，一层为碑阁，阁内四壁嵌有清代杨守敬选刻的《景苏园帖》石刻一百零八块，汇集苏书精品，被世人尊奉为"集苏书之大观"；二层为挹爽楼，得名于苏辙《快哉亭记》中名句"濯长江之清流，挹西山之白云"。放龟亭位于赤壁矶最突出处，亭下岩石壁立，苏轼笔下"乱石穿空，惊涛拍岸，卷起千堆雪"的壮观景象即指此处——昔日江水冲刷的痕迹犹存，白石龟仍踞矶下水中。问鹤亭原名"玩月台"，取苏轼《后赤壁赋》中与孤鹤所化道士梦中对答的描述改名。

苏轼在谪居黄州的四年多时间里，不仅创作了诗、词、文、赋数百篇，而且还留下了大量的书画作品，如《寒食诗帖》《月梅图》等。这些作品在明清时镌刻成碑，镶嵌在东坡赤壁的楼台亭阁之中——书画碑刻总计一百三十九块，居古今名人个人书画碑刻之冠。1985年，日本著名的"东坡迷"山上次郎率"东坡参观访问团"来到东坡赤壁，出于对苏轼的景仰和对东坡赤壁的钟情，山上次郎慨然将珍藏的一卷《寒食帖》复制品捐赠给东坡赤壁管理处——这也是中国大陆唯一的一卷《寒食帖》珍品。如今，东坡赤壁距离长江已有数公里之遥，登最高处的栖霞楼极目远望，也只能看见长江江堤上的葱葱树林；唯有老照片能证明此"赤壁矶"前的道路、池塘、廊道原是滔滔江水。然而，沧海桑田的变化，并不妨碍苏轼"二赋一词"将此地凝为不朽。

West of the ancient city of Huanggang, beyond the Hanchuan Gate, rises a cliff of reddish sandstone — steep as a wall and named "Red Cliff" (Chibi) since the Han dynasty. In the early Western Jin, General Kuai Si built the Hengjiang Hall here to commemorate the Battle of Red Cliff; the Tang poet Du Mu and the Song essayist Wang Yucheng, both exiled to Huanggang, made the cliff's fame grow. In the 3rd year of Yuanfeng, Northern Song (1080), Su Shi was banished to Huangzhou over the "Crow Terrace Poetry Case." During his four-plus years here, he often boated beneath the cliff with friends, and wrote the immortal "Former and Latter Odes to the Red Cliff" and "Nian Nu Jiao: Reminiscence of Red Cliff" — "The great river flows east, washing away all the heroes of a thousand ages..." — making this place world-famous. After Su Shi, a once-remote land became a literary "shrine"; in the late Kangxi reign, the Huanggang prefect Guo Chaocuo renamed it "Dongpo Red Cliff."

The existing buildings, mostly rebuilt in the 7th year of Tongzhi, Qing (1868), cling to the cliff, nestling into the terrain in an irregular quadrilateral. The complex comprises two halls, three towers, two pavilions, one studio, and nine pavilions, blending southern garden charm with official-style elegance. The Er Fu Hall (Two Odes Hall), built in the early Qing and rebuilt in 1868, is the most famous: its central wooden wall, about two zhang high, bears the "Former Red Cliff Ode" in regular script by Cheng Zhizhen on the front, and the "Latter Red Cliff Ode" in a hybrid Han-clerical-and-Wei-stele script by Li Kaishen on the back. The "Two Odes Hall" plaque is in the hand of Li Hongzhang; the front couplet was composed by the revolutionary Huang Xing; inside are also plaques by the Republic's president Xu Shichang.

The Poxian (Immortal Su) Pavilion on the cliff's head has 26 embedded steles, 14 of them by Su Shi himself, including his cursive "Nian Nu Jiao." The Leijiang Pavilion, originally built in the Song as the "Moon-Toast Pavilion," takes its name from the line "one cup to toast the river moon" in the same lyric; it holds 14 steles, notably the Kangxi emperor's calligraphy of the Red Cliff Ode and Ye Zhishen's "one-stroke longevity." The Liuxian (Retaining the Immortal) Pavilion was built in the 10th year of Guangxu (1884) and completed on Su Shi's birthday; the Qixia Tower — one of Song-era Huangzhou's four great towers — bears a plaque by Mao Dun; and the Yishuang Tower's lower floor is the Stele Pavilion, with 108 stones of the "Jingsu Garden Model," selected by Yang Shoujing, hailed as "the grand synthesis of Su's calligraphy." The Fangui (Releasing-the-Turtle) Pavilion, at the cliff's most projecting point, marks the spot where "rugged rocks pierce the sky, raging waves beat the shore, rolling up a thousand heaps of snow" — a white stone turtle still crouches below.

During his exile Su Shi produced not only hundreds of poems and prose pieces, but also many calligraphic and painted works, such as the "Cold Food Poems" and "Plum and Moon"; these were later carved into 139 steles embedded in the pavilions — the most calligraphic steles of any single figure in history. In 1985, the Japanese "Su Shi admirer" Yamagami Jiro donated a reproduction of the "Cold Food Poems" to Dongpo Red Cliff, the only such copy on the Chinese mainland. Today the Yangtze has receded several kilometers from the cliff; yet the "two odes and one lyric" make the place eternal.

鄂州观音阁

Guanyin Pavilion in Ezhou

级别：国家级重点文物保护单位 年代：元至清 地址：鄂州市鄂城区小东门外长江龙蟠矶 类型：宗教建筑·砖瓦木石结构

鄂州观音阁又称龙蟠矶寺，位于鄂州市城区小东门外长江江心龙蟠矶之上，被誉为"万里长江第一阁"。据旧《武昌县志》载："龙蟠矶去岸不盈一里，隆起于江中，周围七十丈。冬出夏没，相传有龙蟠于此石，积日方去，石势蜿蜒如龙，因名。"相传孙权定都武昌（今鄂州）之前，有黄龙蟠卧矶上，积日方去，因而又名"蟠龙石"。万里长江，险称天堑，上起巴蜀，下至吴淞，唯鄂州观音阁雄踞矶头，宛如中流砥柱，气势磅礴——故又被冠以"万里长江第一阁"的美名。

观音阁始建于宋，元至正五年（1345年）由元代蒙古族人监邑铁山在此处用石料堆砌成台，并建成一座寺庙，取名龙蟠矶寺。起初该寺只供奉东方朔，后来又增设了观音像、老君像等，成为佛道两教建筑。明弘治元年（1488年）僧人觉寿募资重修；嘉靖六年（1527年）重修，并加盖了纯阳楼和观澜亭；清同治三年（1864年）湖广总督官文捐资重修观音阁。民国时期一度废毁，后得以重修。1958年被公布为鄂城县文物保护单位，1992年12月被公布为湖北省文物保护单位，2006年5月25日经国务院核定公布为第六批全国重点文物保护单位。

观音阁坐东朝西，逆水而立，阁长二十四米，宽十米，高十四米，基座厚一米余，用长六十公分、宽二十公分、高二十五公分的条石垒成，阁身以青砖砌就，与阁下龙蟠矶巧妙地融为一体，显得雄峻巍峨，气势磅礴。建筑面积约四百平方米（一说三百余平方米），总体为一进纵向三栋，由祖师殿、观音殿、老君殿三殿及纯阳楼、观澜亭等组成，是一处集儒、释、道三教文化于一体的奇特建筑。观音阁下半部那如同船舷样的弧形石墙，既减缓水势，又顺势泄流，任凭江水拍击咆哮，总是稳如泰山——古代鄂州工匠对流体力学的奇妙运用和风格独特的造型艺术，使多少建筑专家驻足留连，赞叹不已。每到汛潮，水漫楼阁，只剩高层窗口，甚至只剩那檐牙、龙吻、中亭在浩淼长江水之中；狂涛巨澜曾使多少名楼华构席卷而去，然而"中流砥柱有蟠龙"（清人官文诗句），汛期一过，水落石出，它又横空出世，威镇江心。

祖师殿为观音阁第一进殿，即前殿，砖木结构，建筑面积五十点五一平方米，四柱九檩。殿内正中偏后处神台供奉祖师鲧——这是少见的以治水英雄鲧为供奉对象的殿宇。神台上方悬挂"虚悬水际"匾额，左右两侧门楣书有"洪波""撼岸"字样，恰切地描绘了观音阁悬于江流之上的姿态。屋脊上悬挂东方朔圆雕小雕像，故又名东方朔殿——相传东方朔能镇压水势，故被供奉于此。南北两侧山墙浮雕青龙、白虎二将；南侧山墙开有一扇侧门，侧门外建有亭角式门楼，门外悬挂匾额"小蓬莱"。正门牌坊匾额上有石刻"龙蟠晓渡"四字门额，出自清代湖广总督官文手笔，下刻嵌"小蓬莱"三字门额。

观音殿即中殿，位于祖师殿的后端，砖木结构，建筑面积四十四点一九平方米，屋面铺小布瓦，抬梁式大木构架，门外左右两侧刻有《重修观音阁记》碑。门两侧设有神台，右侧台上供奉观音雕像，左侧台上雕像已毁；殿内正中偏后设有木雕神龛，龛中供奉有青石阴刻观音大士站像，上方悬挂"普度众生"匾额——是一座佛教殿堂。老君殿即后殿，面阔三间，进深一间，砖木结构，抬梁式大木构架，硬山式屋面，前檐建有一支香轩廊；殿中设有三个牌坊式神龛，龛内供奉有太上老君、玉帝、王母神像；龛上悬挂"紫气东来"四字匾额；龛前左右二山墙柱间刻有八仙图案及其生平小传石刻碑——是一座道教殿堂。三殿合而观之，正好是儒（祖师鲧）、释（观音）、道（老君）三教共处一阁，在万里长江之中仅此一处。

纯阳楼和观澜亭为明嘉靖六年（1527年）增建。观澜亭位于阁西侧挡土墙南端顶部，平面呈三角形，占地一点五平方米，周边修有扶手和栅栏；登亭俯瞰江流，胜景恢宏。纯阳楼内有三间，有睡榻一张，吕洞宾睡像一尊——游人至此，莫不止步屏息，会心一笑：如此蓬莱仙境，独此公得其所哉。阁内还保留有较多的石刻及碑刻：元代的莲花纹刻石古井圈为阁内最早的石刻；碑刻有明代诗人王廷陈的《龙蟠矶》诗三首、刘养微的《龙蟠矶短歌》、光绪年间的《维修观音阁记》《功德碑》题刻等。阁之外墙西壁刻有辛亥革命遗老朱维昌于民国二十一年（1932年）所书"观音阁"三字。

相传，龙蟠矶上原有两个凹口，为"神鳖"的两只巨眼，巨眼时刻注视着江面，使得观音阁能随潮涨潮落；并且两只眼一只出油、一只出盐。后来有一个贪心的道士为了多取油盐，将凹口的口径扩大了数倍，希望出更多的油盐。不料，这一凿把神鳖的两眼戳瞎了，凹口再也不出油盐了，观音阁也不能随潮涨落了。阁中的那一口老井，自古水清见底，水位要高出江水数尺，令人称奇；阁壁间原有一棵老蔻树，一度枯死，后石隙间又萌发数株新枝，近年竟死而复生，正舒展着嫩芽初吐的新枝。这老井、古树和神鳖眼，被称作观音阁"三趣"。观音阁布局得体，构筑精巧，朱槛回廊，重檐飞楹，显示出典型的江南民间建筑艺术特色，在万里长江之中仅此一处。

The Guanyin Pavilion (Guanyin Ge), also called "Longpanji Temple," stands on the Longpanji reef in the middle of the Yangtze River outside the Lesser East Gate of Ezhou City, and is hailed as "the First Pavilion on the Ten-Thousand-Li Yangtze." The old "Wuchang County Gazette" records: "The Longpanji reef rises in the river less than a li from shore, seventy zhang around, emerging in winter and submerging in summer; legend says a dragon coiled upon this stone for days before departing, and the stone winds like a dragon, hence the name." Before Sun Quan established his capital at Wuchang (present-day Ezhou), a yellow dragon was said to have coiled on the reef for days — hence also the name "Coiled Dragon Stone."

A small temple existed on the reef in the Song dynasty; in the 5th year of Zhizheng, Yuan (1345), the Mongol supervisor Tieshan had a stone platform built and a temple erected, named "Longpanji Temple," initially dedicated to Dongfang Shuo. Statues of Guanyin and Laozi were later added, making it a Buddhist-Daoist complex. The Ming monk Jueshou raised funds to restore it in 1488; in 1527 the Chunyang Tower and Guanlan Pavilion were added; in 1864 the Huguang Governor-General Guan Wen funded a major restoration. In 1958 it became an Ezhou County Cultural Heritage Site, in 1992 a Hubei Provincial site, and in 2006 a National Key Cultural Heritage Site.

The pavilion faces west against the current, 24 meters long, 10 meters wide, 14 meters high, on a stone base over a meter thick. The curved lower wall, like a ship's hull, deflects and channels the floodwaters — a remarkable application of fluid mechanics by ancient Ezhou craftsmen. At flood season the river submerges the hall, leaving only the upper windows, eaves, and central pavilion in the swirling water; yet when the waters recede, the pavilion reappears, "a coiled dragon as the pillar mid-current" (Governor Guan Wen's own words).

The complex, about 400 square meters, comprises the Zushi (Patriarch) Hall, Guanyin Hall, and Laojun Hall, plus the Chunyang Tower and Guanlan Pavilion. The Zushi Hall (50.51 sq m), the front hall, enshrines Gun, the flood-control hero — a rare dedication — beneath a plaque "Suspended Over the Waters," with side lintels inscribed "Flood Wave" and "Shaking Bank"; a small carved Dongfang Shuo on the ridge gives it the alternate name "Dongfang Shuo Hall." The Guanyin Hall (44.19 sq m), the middle hall, houses a stone-carved standing Guanyin in a wooden shrine beneath a "Salvation of All Beings" plaque — a Buddhist hall. The Laojun Hall, the rear hall, holds statues of Taishang Laojun, the Jade Emperor, and the Queen Mother in three shrine-altars, with a "Purple Aura from the East" plaque and wall carvings of the Eight Immortals — a Daoist hall. The three halls together represent Confucian (Gun), Buddhist (Guanyin), and Daoist (Laozi) faiths coexisting under one roof — unique on the entire Yangtze.

The Chunyang Tower, added in 1527, holds a reclining statue of Lü Dongbin. The oldest surviving carved stone is a Yuan-dynasty lotus-pattern well ring; steles include three "Longpanji" poems by the Ming poet Wang Tingchen. The west wall bears the three characters "Guanyin Ge," inscribed in 1932 by the Xinhai veteran Zhu Weichang. Legend says the reef once had two hollows — the eyes of a "divine turtle" — one producing oil, one salt; a greedy priest enlarged them to get more, and blinded the turtle, after which the oil and salt ceased and the pavilion could no longer rise and fall with the tide. The old well, the dead-but-revived tree, and the divine turtle's eyes are called the "Three Wonders" of Guanyin Pavilion.

唐崖土司城遗址

Tangya Tusi Domain

级别：世界文化遗产·国家级重点文物保护单位 年代：元至清 地址：恩施州咸丰县唐崖镇唐崖司村 类型：古城址·土司遗址

唐崖土司是元、明至清初时期鄂西"九溪十八峒"著名土司之一，为咸丰四大土司之最。从元朝至正十五年（1355年）建立，至清朝雍正十三年（1735年）"改土归流"废止，共历16代18位土司，计381年，先后14次被朝廷征调，多次褒奖，赐建土司王城，唐崖土司王城也成为元、明、清三代土家族覃氏土司政权的统治中心。唐崖土司城址位于咸丰县唐崖镇唐崖司村，背依玄武山、面临唐崖河，遗址所在地古属施州，主要族群为自古定居于此的土家族。城址占地总面积七十四万平方米，是全国规模最大、保存最完整、最具有民族特色的土司文化遗址，素有"小故宫"之称。2006年被国务院公布为第六批全国重点文物保护单位，2015年7月4日，在德国波恩召开的联合国教科文组织第39届世界遗产大会上，作为"中国土司遗址"申遗项目组成部分，成功列入世界文化遗产名录，成为湖北省第三处、恩施州第一处世界文化遗产。

唐崖土司城格局清晰，功能完备，遗存类型包括城防设施（城墙、城门等）、交通设施（道路、桥梁、码头等）、建筑遗址、墓葬、苑囿、手工业遗址（采石场）等多种类型。城内由一条南北向主干道、三纵三横的次干道及数十条巷道组成的道路系统分割为三十余个院落。道路体系共分为"三街十八巷"，由主干道、三横三纵的次干道、数十条巷道构成。其主干道南北向贯通全城，依次分为上、中、下三街，全长近八百八十米，路面采用宽一点五至三点五米不等青砂岩铺筑，是目前我国保存最为完整的明代石板道路。城内包括小衙门、大寺堂等院落在内，共有院落三十六个，水井遗迹十七处。大部分院落由围墙、门道构成，为一般生活区。

衙署区遗址位于城址核心区的中部偏西，坐西朝东，中轴对称，前朝后寝式布局，形状大致呈长方形，东西长二百一十八米，南北平均宽一百五十五米，总面积约三万三千七百九十平方米。主体建筑利用山势坡地，依次分布在自东向西的五级台地之上，地势逐渐升高，沿东西轴线依次为"荆南雄镇"牌坊、大衙门遗址、官言堂遗址及附属建筑、内宅，建筑皆朝东。"荆南雄镇"牌坊建于明天启四年（1624年），是朝廷为表彰第十二代土司覃鼎的战功而立，为唐崖土司城遗址中最具标志性的建筑遗存。牌坊为砂石仿木结构，高七点一五米，通宽八点零四米，三开间，四根石柱前后有高二点五米的抱鼓石及石狮。横额中两面分别书写"荆南雄镇"和"楚蜀屏翰"八个阴刻大字。中间枋面雕刻"土王出巡""哪吒闹海"等题材，两侧枋面雕刻"渔樵耕读、槐荫送子"及麒麟、龙、凤等图案——这些题材多取自汉地，但有本地变通，也有直接反映土司生活的"土王出巡"等，是汉土艺术结合的产物。

张王庙又称桓侯庙，位于城址西北部，始建于明万历年间，为唐崖土司城武庙，因庙内主祀三国名将张飞而得名。庙内存石人石马两对，石马一公一母，左侧公马高二点三八米，右侧母马高二点零八米，长二点五米，雕凿精细，栩栩如生，马身鞍、镫、缰、辔清晰可辨。马缰上阴刻文字记万历三十九年（1611年）由印官田夫人、峒主覃杰携子覃文仲修立。庙内还存有石刻文字六处，包括两条石马铭刻和四方重修庙宇碑刻，记录了清乾隆八年（1743年）、嘉庆十二年（1807年）和光绪四年（1878年）三次重修张王庙的历史。土王墓（皇坟）封土保存完好，墓葬石室宏大而华丽。椁室以钻凿砂岩垒成，前观为仿木结构的一斗三升式重檐建筑，长七米，五根廊柱形成四间墓室，廊顶雕刻圆形藻井饰。墓前为八字形祭台，前端各立一小兽，两侧饰以精雕麒麟，高约一米——整个王坟以石建构，十分精美，代表了这一时期的石雕工艺水平。田氏夫人墓在土王墓左侧，墓前立有石碑和石牌坊，碑面中刻"明显妣诰封武略将军覃太夫人田氏之墓"，前记"孝男印官覃宗尧祀"，后题"皇明崇祯岁庚午季夏吉旦立"。城址西部玄武山顶有夫妻杉两株，相传为土司覃鼎夫妇所植，树龄已超过四百年，棵围约五米，高约四十四米，冠幅二百二十五平方米，两树枝干连理，并峙而立，如夫妻携手，是唐崖土司城址自然景观的象征和标志。唐崖土司城址的最大价值在于清晰、完整，它是西南地区格局最清晰、保存最完整的土司城址之一，见证了古代中国作为统一多民族国家对西南多民族地区独特的"齐政修教、因俗而治"管理智慧。

The Tangya Tusi Domain, a World Heritage Site since 2015, is the largest, best-preserved, and most ethnically distinctive Tusi (native chieftain) cultural site in China. Located in Tangya Town, Xianfeng County, Enshi, it nestles against Xuanwu Mountain and faces the Tangya River. The Tangya Tusi — a Tujia chieftain lineage surnamed Qin — ruled this area for 381 years, through 16 generations and 18 chieftains, from 1355 in the Yuan dynasty until the "Gaitu Guiliu" (bureaucratization of native chieftains) in 1735 during the Qing. The city was the political, military, economic, and cultural center of a 600-square-kilometer domain, and is popularly called the "Little Forbidden City."

The 740,000-square-meter site preserves a clear, complete urban layout: three main streets, eighteen lanes, and thirty-six courtyards, connected by a road system totaling 880 meters of flagstone-paved main thoroughfare — the best-preserved Ming dynasty stone road in China. The administrative compound (yashu) sits in the western core, 218 × 155 meters, rising on five terraces along the central axis. Its gateway, the "Jingnan Xiongzhen" (Mighty Bastion South of Jing) archway, built in 1624 to honor the 12th chieftain Qin Ding's military merits, bears inscriptions on both faces and relief carvings blending Han and Tujia artistic traditions. The Zhang Wang Temple (Duke Zhang's Shrine, dedicated to Zhang Fei) preserves two pairs of stone horses and grooms carved in 1611, with inscriptions recording their donors. The chieftain's tomb (Huangfen) is a stone masterpiece with a one-bracket-three-lift eaved facade and circular ceiling caisson; beside it stands the tomb of Lady Tian, dated 1630. Two "Husband-and-Wife" cedars on Xuanwu Peak, planted over 400 years ago by Chieftain Qin Ding and his wife, reach 44 meters and symbolize the site.

The Tangya Tusi Domain's supreme value lies in its clarity and completeness: it is the most legible and best-preserved Tusi city site in southwest China, witnessing the ancient Chinese empire's management wisdom of "governing according to local customs" in multi-ethnic frontier regions — a management approach that promoted sustained regional development and contributed to long-term national unity.

鱼木寨

Yumu Village (Fish-Wood Stronghold)

级别：国家级重点文物保护单位 年代：明、清 地址：恩施州利川市谋道镇鱼木村 类型：古城址·土司军事要塞

鱼木寨，又名鱼目寨、舆木寨，是一座土家族人村寨，位于湖北省利川市谋道镇西边、鄂渝交界的莽莽大山中，东距利川市六十一公里。山寨孤悬山崖顶端，寨上村庄海拔一千三百余米，四周悬崖峭壁，沟壑纵横，几乎与外界隔绝，唯寨西南有一处宽约两米的石板古道与外界相通。清同治五年（1866年）增修《万县志》载："鱼木寨山高峻，四周壁立，广约十里，形如鼗鼓，以鼓柄入寨门，其径险仄。"占地约六平方公里，寨内居住着五百多户土家山民——这里保存着土家古堡、雄关、古墓、栈道和民宅，是国内保存最为完好的土家山寨，素有"天下第一土家古寨""世外桃源"之美称。2006年被列为第六批全国重点文物保护单位。

鱼木寨始建时间可追溯到明洪武二年（1369年）。据考，龙渊安抚司谭福一于明洪武二年落业万县乐往坝，洪武八年（1375年），谭元亨袭龙渊安抚司职，改为龙阳峒安抚司，占据铜锣关、鱼木寨一带地方，建关设卡。明万历十四年（1586年）编入万县，1955年12月由四川万县划归湖北利川。清嘉庆元年（1796年），川鄂陕边境爆发大规模白莲教起义，义军活动频繁，让偏居一隅闭关自守的鱼木寨很是不安。时川鄂联防团总谭登杰倡议在鱼木寨建寨，得到寨民积极响应——一百二十名寨民自愿捐资，全体寨民出力，终于在清嘉庆四年（1799年，一说1801年）堡寨竣工。

关于"鱼木寨"之名的由来，有一段广为流传的传说。相传古代马、谭两大土司连年征战，攻寨一方久攻不下——一说守寨的是马土司，攻寨的是谭土司；也有反过来说的。一日，守寨土司从山寨上抛下活鱼无数，有的鱼就落在攻寨土司帐前的树枝上。攻寨土司见鱼兴叹："吾克此寨，犹如缘木求鱼也！"——鱼木寨由此得名。抛开历史真相不论，正是因为有天堑之势，鱼木寨形成守卫的天然屏障，才使得这里免遭战火涂炭。鱼木寨的发现也有一段传奇：1982年仲夏的一个中午，鱼木寨上雷声阵阵，忽然一个闷雷击在主寨楼上，当时住在主寨楼上的成氏父女和一个躲雨的山民被击身亡——这一雷击事件引起了人们的注意，人们意外地发现，这个人口仅四百来人的村，民风淳朴，风情浓郁，竟是一座保存十分完整的土家古寨。

主寨楼是入寨的第一关，气势磅礴。寨楼建在名曰"金扁担"的悬崖脊上，当地人叫作"寨颈"，耸立在绝壁汇合的脊背上，两面是垂直高度达五百米的悬崖。寨楼建于清嘉庆年间，前小后大呈梯形，楼高近七米，前为防御区，分上下楼，一楼全用墩子堆砌实在，中有一门供人进出，二楼有观察射击孔洞；后为守防人员生活住宅区。"悬崖脊上建寨楼，一夫把关鬼神愁"——寨门一关，断绝了进寨的唯一通道。古栈道三阳关隐于老崖隙中，整个山寨一个寨门进、一个卡门出，独特的地物地貌国内罕见。寨上道路险要，"亮梯子"建于绝壁之上，每级均用条石一头插入岩壁、一头悬空建成；"手扒岩"则直接在石壁上凿坑——正所谓"蜀道难，难于上青天"。寨上还现存古人居住、织布、榨油、铸币崖穴近一百处，文化层深厚。

鱼木寨的墓葬文化独树一帜。寨内现存大型清代碑墓十座，碑高一般都在五米以上，墓石石雕工艺精湛。其中"双寿居"集马派雕刻艺术之大成，几百名工匠雕凿三年，雕出了六出戏剧故事中的五百多个人物造像，实为稀有艺术珍品。碑林中的石碑造型有塔式、牌坊式、牌楼多层式、圈顶式、平卧式等多种——有关专家考证后认为，鱼木寨堪称世界土家文化的遗留中心。寨内以木建筑为主，青石与青石、木材与木材、木材与青石之间的结合，均用古老巴国传统的营造方法，以榫头相联，阴阳相扣。寨内石制用品种类繁多，包括石碾、石磨、石缸、石盆、石桌、石凳——寨上人亲切地称青石为"石宝"。2006年鱼木寨被列为全国重点文物保护单位后，利川市检察院还开展了少数民族传统文化保护专项行动，对盗掘"成乾春墓"案件进行查处，古墓被盗洞修补并安装监控设备进行保护。

Yumu Village (Fish-Wood Stronghold), also called Yumu or Yumu Zhai, is a Tujia village perched on a clifftop at over 1,300 meters elevation, at the Hubei-Chongqing border, 61 km east of Lichuan. The village is isolated on all sides by sheer cliffs, accessible only by a two-meter-wide flagstone path on the southwest. The Qing-era "Wanxian Gazette" describes it: "The mountain is high and steep, walled on all sides, about ten li across, shaped like a drum, with the handle forming the gate — its path is perilous and narrow." Covering about six square kilometers with over 500 Tujia households, it is China's best-preserved Tujia stronghold, hailed as the "First Tujia Ancient Village" and a "World Apart."

The stronghold's origins trace to 1369 in the Ming, when the Longyuan Pacification Commissioner Tan Fuyi settled nearby; his successor renamed it the Longyang Dong Pacification Office and occupied the Tongluo Pass and Yumu Village area. In 1796, the White Lotus Rebellion swept the region, and the local militia leader Tan Dengjie mobilized 120 villagers to fortify the clifftop, completing the main stronghold tower in 1799-1801. The name "Yumu" (Fish-Wood) comes from a legend: when one chieftain besieged the other, the defender threw live fish down from the cliff; the attacker, seeing fish hanging in trees, sighed: "Taking this stronghold is like climbing a tree to catch fish" — "缘木求鱼" (yuanmu qiuyu), hence "Yumu."

The main stronghold tower, built on a cliff-ridge called "Golden Carrying-Pole," rises seven meters with a lower defense floor of solid masonry and a single entrance, and an upper observation/shooting floor, flanked by 500-meter drops on both sides. The Three-Sun Pass (Sanyang Guan), hidden in a cliff crevice, is a stone-paved path with steps cantilevered from the rock face — "Liangtizi" (Bright Ladder) and "Shoupayan" (Hand-Scramble Rock) — embodying the saying "The Shu road is hard, harder than climbing to heaven." The village preserves nearly 100 cliff caves once used for dwelling, weaving, oil-pressing, and coin-casting, and ten large Qing dynasty carved-stone tombs, the grandest being "Shuangshouju" (Twin Longevity Dwelling), on which hundreds of craftsmen worked for three years to carve over 500 figures from six operas. The village's wooden structures use ancient Ba-state mortise-and-tenon joinery; its ubiquitous blue-stone implements — mills, presses, vats, basins — are affectionately called "Stone Treasures."

古德寺

Gude Temple

级别：国家级重点文物保护单位 年代：清至民国 地址：武汉市江岸区工农兵路24号 类型：宗教建筑·钢筋混凝土结构

第一眼看到古德寺中的圆通宝殿，很容易感到困惑：入口门廊的火焰拱券和屋顶的塔林，墙面上的尖拱券和玫瑰花窗——这真的是一座佛教寺院吗？事实上，这样奇特的大殿，不仅在武汉独树一帜，在汉传佛教的寺庙建筑中也是独一无二，被游客和网友赞为"汉传佛教第一奇观""一眼看尽中西文明的建筑孤本""万国建筑博物馆"。古德寺位于武汉市汉口黄浦路上滑坡路七十四号，与归元寺、宝通寺、莲溪寺并称武汉四大佛教丛林。"古德"一名取义"心性好古，普度以德"。2013年，古德寺被列入第七批全国重点文物保护单位。

清光绪三年（1877年），汉口解放大道东段、黄浦路北段一带还是荒凉的坡地，一位法号隆希的游方僧人在这里建了一座小寺，起名"古德茅蓬"。彼时，汉口已开埠十五年，早被誉为"楚中第一繁盛处"，但黄浦路和后湖一带仍是沼泽地和土墩子，人烟稀少。1905年，汉阳归元寺昌宏法师因仰慕隆希学识，常到古德茅蓬参学。1909年，隆希自感年事已高，选择昌宏做了接班人，古德茅蓬也迎来了第一次改扩建。1911年10月，武昌起义爆发，京汉铁路刘家庙车站周边成了战场——刘家庙车站与古德寺直线距离仅一公里多。弥漫的硝烟中，昌宏率领僧众砍伐寺院竹林制成担架，救护起义军伤员，同时率众在寺后菜园收殓大量起义军遗体。战后有三千余具烈士遗骨埋葬在寺侧。1912年4月，孙中山专程到古德寺凭吊革命烈士；1914年，时任副总统的黎元洪改"古德茅蓬"为"古德寺"，并亲笔竖题寺名——自古以来，竖写匾额是皇家寺庙的专享，古德寺因在辛亥革命中的功绩获此殊荣。古德寺从此声名远播，香火鼎盛，成为"全镇各寺之冠"。

昌宏抓住机遇，于1921年秋再次大规模扩建庙宇，主持推倒了原有的大雄宝殿，开始建造一座构思奇特、融合东西方多种建筑元素的圆通宝殿。圆通宝殿为上塔下寺造型，采用框架体系，四方形，四方开门，内高十六米、外高二十五米，殿基正方形，边长三十五米，十分开阔大气，建筑面积一千多平方米。殿顶的大小佛塔共九座，象征汉传佛教五佛四菩萨，也与藏传佛教曼荼罗布局相似；站在地面看塔顶，最多只能看到七座，暗合了中国道教"北斗九星、七显二隐"的说法。殿顶九十六个莲花方墩，源自中国传统望柱，寓意"国之四维，天圆地方"；塔四周设有佛教中国化后的二十四诸天护法神像。

圆通宝殿最令人叹为观止之处在于它融合了欧亚多种宗教建筑风格：大殿外墙上镶嵌的尖拱窗、玫瑰花窗类似欧洲哥特式教堂的玻璃花窗，带来开阔明亮的光影效果；外廊的方形立柱顶部装饰着卷涡纹和繁复的卷草花纹，借鉴了古希腊神庙的爱奥尼柱和柯林斯柱；大门花瓣样的拱券带有伊斯兰清真寺风格；殿顶佛塔融合了中式廊亭和缅甸佛寺群塔样式；正墙上的狮头、象头、孔雀、大鹏金翅鸟浮雕则源于风靡南印度和东南亚的帕纳瓦风格。九座佛塔的塔刹，既像风向标又像十字架，在中国塔文化中独树一帜。神奇之处在于，这些来自欧洲、东南亚以及中国本土的建筑符号和装饰元素并非生硬拼凑——它们被巧妙地组织在传统中式的方形框架和空间秩序内，共同服务于佛教殿堂的核心功能。古德寺混合了欧亚宗教建筑的特色，融大乘、小乘和藏密三大佛教流派于一身，为汉传佛教唯一、世界仅存两座此类风格的佛教建筑之一。华中科技大学建筑与城市规划学院副教授万谦推测，圆通宝殿的设计可能出自当时汉口租界的欧洲建筑设计所，是西方文化汇入武汉后文化交融与经济发展的产物。建造圆通宝殿的工程处正是修筑武汉大学珞珈山的"汉协盛营造厂"——武汉三镇三百多座著名建筑中，建筑商可考者一百零七处，其中五十六处为"汉协盛营造厂"建造。据1935年《益世报》记载，费用将近二十万元——在当时是一笔巨款。古德寺保存的《昌宏老和尚略传》记载：圆通宝殿建成后，昌宏心愿已了，回房打坐七日，安然圆寂。

1931年夏，武汉连续三个月阴雨连绵，"鄂渚之三镇，昔日繁华，顿成泽国"——一张老照片显示，洪水之中圆通宝殿孤零零矗立，因其钢筋混凝土结构而幸存。1954年10月19日，周恩来总理陪印度总理尼赫鲁参观古德寺，尼赫鲁对武汉拥有一座印度帕纳瓦风格的群塔寺庙感到惊异。2013年，第二届武汉设计双年展公布五座"武汉城市地标"，经网民投票，古德寺以十万四千一百五十五票从江汉路水塔、湖北省图书馆、晴川阁等建筑中脱颖而出，位列城市地标之首。从沼泽孤岛上的"古德茅蓬"，到声名远播的"各寺之冠"，再到汉口闹市一隅的"汉传佛教第一奇观"，历经百年沧桑，这座古刹始终敞开怀抱，容纳着各种元素的碰撞与共生——它开放包容的胸襟、务实求变的智慧与超越时代的文化自觉，给这座城市留下了一个鲜明的符号。

At first sight of the Yuantong Treasure Hall (Yuandong Baodian) at Gude Temple, one is bewildered: flame-arch portals and rooftop pagoda cluster, pointed arches and rose windows — is this really a Buddhist temple? Indeed, such a singular hall is unique not only in Wuhan but in all Chinese Buddhist (Han-school) temple architecture, hailed as the "First Wonder of Han Buddhism," a "building orphan that reveals Chinese and Western civilizations in a single glance," and a "Museum of World Architecture." Gude Temple, on Huangpu Road in Hankou, is one of Wuhan's Four Great Buddhist Monasteries. The name "Gude" derives from the maxim "Cherish the ancient in heart, save all beings with virtue." In 2013 it was designated a National Key Cultural Heritage Site.

In the 3rd year of Guangxu, Qing (1877), the wandering monk Longxi built a small temple called "Gude Mao Peng" (Ancient Virtue Thatched Hut) on what was then desolate high ground near present-day Huangpu Road. In 1909, Longxi passed the robe to Changhong of Guiyuan Temple. When the 1911 Wuchang Uprising erupted, the Liujiadamiao station — just over a kilometer away — became a battlefield; Changhong led his monks through gunfire to rescue wounded revolutionaries and bury 3,000 fallen soldiers behind the temple. In 1912, Sun Yat-sen came to pay respects; in 1914, Vice President Li Yuanhong renamed it "Gude Temple" and inscribed the name vertically — a privilege reserved for imperial temples — in recognition of its revolutionary merits. The temple flourished as "the foremost of all temples in the town."

In 1921, Changhong demolished the old main hall and began building the extraordinary Yuantong Treasure Hall: a square, four-doored reinforced-concrete structure, 16 meters interior height, 25 meters exterior, on a 35-meter-square base of over 1,000 square meters. Nine rooftop pagodas symbolize the Five Buddhas and Four Bodhisattvas; from the ground, at most seven are visible — a Daoist allusion to the "Nine Stars of the Big Dipper, seven visible and two hidden." Ninety-six lotus pedestals embody "the four pillars of the state, round heaven and square earth"; twenty-four Heavenly Guardian figures encircle the towers. The hall fuses Gothic rose windows and pointed arches, Greek Ionic and Corinthian columns, Islamic petal-arch portals, Burmese-Ananda-style pagoda clusters, and South Indian Pallava reliefs of lions, elephants, peacocks, and garudas. These diverse elements are not haphazardly assembled but organized within a traditional Chinese square framework — making Gude Temple the only Han Buddhist building, and one of only two in the world, to integrate Mahayana, Theravada, and Tibetan Vajrayana in a single hall.

The contractor was the Hanxie Sheng Construction Company, builder of 56 of Wuhan's 107 documented modern landmarks, including Wuhan University's Luojia campus; the cost approached 200,000 silver dollars. In the great 1931 Wuhan flood, the reinforced-concrete hall stood alone amid the waters. In 1954, Premier Zhou Enlai accompanied Indian Prime Minister Nehru on a visit — Nehru marveled at finding a Pallava-style temple-cluster in inland China. In 2013, Gude Temple was voted the top "Wuhan City Landmark" by 104,155 netizen votes. From a thatched hut in a swamp to "foremost of all temples" to today's "First Wonder of Han Buddhism" in a bustling city, this century-old temple has always embraced the collision and symbiosis of diverse cultures — a symbol of openness, pragmatism, and cultural foresight.

玉泉寺及铁塔

Yuquan Temple and Iron Pagoda

级别：国家级重点文物保护单位 年代：东汉至清 地址：宜昌市当阳市玉泉山东麓 类型：宗教建筑·砖木、铁铸结构

玉泉寺位于湖北省当阳市城西南十二公里的玉泉山东麓，是中国佛教天台宗的四大祖庭之一，也是中国佛教历史最为悠久的寺院之一，被誉为"荆楚丛林之冠""三楚名山"。早在东汉建安年间，汜水关镇国寺的普净禅师云游至此，便在这里结茅修行，初名普净庵。《三国演义》第七十七回"玉泉山关公显圣，洛阳城曹操感神"中，文中喝破大叫"还我头来"的关羽冤魂的，正是这位普净禅师——而正史中夷陵之战时，刘备也曾亲临当阳为关羽祭祀，并兴建显圣祠。南北朝时，梁宣帝因玉泉山形如一只倒置的大船，在此敕建覆船山寺；隋开皇十三年（593年），天台宗实际创始人智者大师（智顗）奉诏建寺，改名为玉泉寺，成为智者大师的道场——其重要代表著作《摩诃止观》《法华玄义》均在玉泉讲演结集，在中国佛教史上留下"东土释迦""九旬谈妙"等佳话。晋王杨广赐额"玉泉寺"。唐代，禅宗"北渐"神秀大师曾从黄梅东山寺（今五祖寺）来此弘扬禅法二十余年，朝野钦重，玉泉寺从此逐渐兴盛，成为海内外佛教徒景仰的圣地。北宋真宗天禧年间，由于明肃皇后信仰佛教，玉泉寺一度达到鼎盛规模——"为楼者九，为殿者十八，三千七百僧舍"，常驻千余僧人。当时，玉泉寺与浙江天台国清寺、山东长清灵岩寺和江苏南京栖霞寺并称为"天下丛林四绝"。1982年，玉泉寺及铁塔被国务院公布为第二批全国重点文物保护单位。

玉泉寺也是伽蓝菩萨道场、关公信仰的发源地。据唐《重修玉泉关庙记》载：智者大师至覆船山一乔木下趺坐入定，见一人前致敬，云："予即关某，死有余烈，故王此山。禅师何枉法足？"又言："弟子愿与子平建寺。"寺成，关公向大师乞受归戒——由此，关公成为保护伽蓝、护正祛邪的护法神，并为后人尊为"武财神"。现玉泉寺山麓尚有"汉云长显圣处"石望表（明代所立），以及全国最早的关庙"显烈祠"（始建于南北朝前）。北宋大中祥符年间，宋真宗派官专程到玉泉寺祭祀关公——这是见于史籍最早的官祀记载。此外，玉泉寺高僧辈出：鉴真大师曾在此从弘景律师受具足戒，后东渡日本传教；一行本在此研习天台教观与戒律，后成为中国古代著名天文学家和密宗一代宗师；北宋天禧年间，道源于玉泉山传灯录院编撰完成中国禅宗重要历史文献《景德传灯录》。

玉泉寺坐西朝东，依山而建，中轴对称布局。主要建筑布置在一条东西轴线上，由东而西有天王殿、大雄宝殿、毗卢殿三大建筑。大雄宝殿为寺内主体建筑，始建于隋开皇十三年（593年），历代重修，是中国南方最大的一座古建筑。大殿面阔七间四十一米，进深五间二十四米，高二十三米，重檐歇山顶，抬梁式构架，立柱七十二根，全部是金丝楠木，采用穿斗与抬梁相结合的手法，榫卯结构。殿中斗拱分内槽、外槽二种，共一百五十四朵；天花板九十一块，每块一点六六米见方，上面用矿物颜色粘贴彩画，种类有火珠、云龙、莲荷。屋面为重檐歇山灰色筒板瓦顶，正脊高一点三米，以四十余块灰陶花版拼成，两面各有高浮雕五条腾云驾雾的蛟龙。殿上檐悬"智者道场"直匾，是中国佛教协会原会长赵朴初的手笔。1982年至1984年，国家文物局拨款全落架大修。殿前有铸于隋代的大型铁镬、元代铁釜和铁钟；殿侧有石刻观音画像一通，传为唐代画圣吴道子手迹。寺中还保存一株唐代银杏，树龄一千八百多年，树高三十四米，胸径三点二米。

玉泉铁塔位于玉泉寺前方土丘上，本名"佛牙舍利塔"，俗称"棱金铁塔""千佛塔"，铸于北宋嘉祐六年（1061年），为重瘗唐高宗、则天皇后所授舍利而铸建。铁塔为仿木构楼阁式，八角十三层，通高十六点九四五米，重二万六千四百七十二公斤——是我国现存最高、最重、最完整的古铁塔。据塔铭记载，共用铁"七万六千六百斤"。铁塔由地宫、塔基、塔身、塔刹四部分组成。地宫为石质六角形竖井，内置汉白玉须弥座，座上置石函三重，函中供奉舍利。塔基、塔身均为生铁铸造：双层须弥座，铁铸塔座八面铸有铁围山、大海、八仙过海、二龙戏珠及石榴花饰纹，座八隅各铸顶塔力士一尊，全身甲胄，脚踏仙山托顶塔座，体态刚健，状极威猛。塔身各层出平座，置斗拱出檐，底层柱角铸八个力士，走马板上铸有二龙戏珠、八仙过海、海山、水波、海藻等浮雕图案；相间诸面各设壶门龛和佛像。角梁飞檐的前端铸有凌空的龙头，用以悬挂风铎。第二层东、南、西、北四面分别铸有塔名、塔重、铸建年代、工匠姓名及有关事迹——塔身铸有铭文一千三百九十七字，铸有佛像二千二百七十九尊，俨然一幅铁铸佛国世界图。塔刹为铜质（清代道光年间以黄铜重铸），形似宝葫芦。铁塔通体不施榫扣，不加焊粘，逐件叠压，自重以固——其外型俊秀挺拔，稳健玲珑，如玉笋嵌空。每值夕阳照射塔身，紫气金霞交相辉映，"铁塔棱金"蔚为奇观，为玉泉古八景之一。九百多年前铸造此塔时尚无起重设备，全靠人力逐层叠装；千年不锈，又是一奇。玉泉铁塔对研究中国古代冶金铸造、金属防腐、营造法式、建筑力学、铸雕艺术以及佛教史具有十分重要的价值。

— 英文 English —

Yuquan Temple, on the eastern foot of Yuquan Mountain twelve kilometers southwest of Dangyang, is one of the four great ancestral courts of the Tiantai school and one of China's most ancient Buddhist temples, hailed as the "Crown of Jing-Chu Monastic Forests" and one of the "Three Great Mountains of Chu." As early as the Jian'an period of Eastern Han, the monk Pujing arrived and built a thatched hermitage — the same Pujing who, in Chapter 77 of "Romance of the Three Kingdoms," breaks through Guan Yu's ghostly cry of "Return my head!" After the Battle of Yiling, Liu Bei personally came to Dangyang to sacrifice for Guan Yu and built the "Manifestation Shrine." In the Northern and Southern Dynasties, Emperor Xuan of Liang built "Fuchuan Mountain Temple" here (the mountain resembles an overturned boat); in the 13th year of Kaihuang, Sui (593), the Tiantai founder Master Zhiyi built the temple and renamed it Yuquan Temple, where he composed his masterworks the "Mohe Zhiguan" and "Fahua Xuanyi." In the Tang, the Northern Chan master Shenxiu came from Huangmei's Fifth Patriarch Temple to teach for over twenty years. At its Song peak under Emperor Zhenzong and Empress Mingsu, the temple had "nine towers, eighteen halls, and 3,700 monks' quarters," and was ranked with Guoqing, Lingyan, and Qixia temples as the "Four Wonders of the World's Monastic Forests." In 1982 it was designated a National Key Cultural Heritage Site.

Yuquan Temple is also the birthplace of Guan Yu worship. According to the Tang-era "Record of Rebuilding Yuquan Guan Shrine," when Master Zhiyi sat in meditation beneath a great tree, Guan Yu's spirit appeared, offered his mountain as a temple site, and requested Buddhist precepts — becoming the guardian deity of the sangharama (qielan), later revered as the "God of War and Wealth." The temple still has the Ming-era "Place of Guan Yu's Manifestation" stone pillar and the "Xianlie Shrine," China's earliest Guan Yu temple (pre-Northern and Southern Dynasties). In the Dazhong Xiangfu period of Northern Song, Emperor Zhenzong sent officials to offer sacrifices at Yuquan — the earliest recorded official sacrifice to Guan Yu. The temple produced many eminent monks: Jianzhen received full precepts here before his eastward journey to Japan; the astronomer-monk Yixing studied Tiantai doctrine and became a Vajrayana master; and the "Jingde Record of the Lamp" — a foundational Chan Buddhist chronicle — was compiled here.

The temple faces east against the mountain, with a symmetrical central axis. The Mahavira Hall, first built in 593, is the largest ancient building in southern China — seven bays wide (41 m), five deep (24 m), 23 m high, with a double-eave flush-hipped roof and 72 golden-phoebe (nanmu) columns in mortise-and-tenon construction. Its 154 bracket sets, 91 painted ceiling panels, and grey-tile roof with dragon-embossed ceramic ridge are all hallmarks of Ming official architecture. The plaque "Sage's Dojo" is in the hand of Zhao Puchu. Before the hall stand Sui and Yuan dynasty iron vessels; beside it, a Guanyin stone engraving attributed to the Tang painter Wu Daozi.

The Yuquan Iron Pagoda, before the temple, was cast in the 6th year of Jiayou, Northern Song (1061) to re-enshrine relics bestowed by Emperor Gaozong and Empress Wu Zetian. An octagonal, thirteen-story wood-imitation pavilion-style structure, it stands 16.945 meters tall and weighs 26,472 kg — China's tallest, heaviest, and most complete ancient iron pagoda. Its double-base, iron-cast body bears relief panels of the Eight Immortals, twin dragons, and sea motifs, eight armored atlas-figures at the base corners, 1,397 characters of inscription, and 2,279 Buddha images — a cast-iron Buddhist realm. Wind-bells hang from dragon-headed eave corners. The copper finial was recast in the Daoguang reign. The entire tower uses no mortise, no welding — each piece is held by gravity alone. At sunset, the tower glows with purple and golden light — "Iron Pagoda's Golden Glow," one of Yuquan's Eight Scenes. Cast over 900 years ago without cranes, assembled by human power, and still rust-free — it is a treasure of Chinese metallurgy, structural mechanics, and Buddhist art.

黄陵庙

Huangling Temple

级别：国家级重点文物保护单位 年代：明万历四十六年（1618）重建 地址：宜昌市夷陵区三斗坪镇黄牛岩山麓 类型：祭祀建筑·穿斗式木结构

黄陵庙古称黄牛庙、黄牛祠，坐落于长江三峡西陵峡中段黄牛岩下的九龙山麓。黄牛峡是西陵峡"四峡"之一，江水翻滚不息，波涛汹涌，两岸山势高耸，粗犷多变——庙就建在黄牛峡南岸的一方形台地上。这里人称"九龙捧圣"（亦作"九龙捧珠"）：从黄牛岩下延出九条山岭，形如九条游龙，而龙头共同捧向的位置，正是黄陵庙所在。庙上距三峡大坝坝址约七公里，下距葛洲坝水利枢纽三十五公里，坐南朝北，依山面江。庙前设有神牛广场，倚栏远眺，上下西陵峡的奇丽风光尽收眼底，岸边的垂钓者安然端坐，竿入水中——浑然不觉身后这座古庙的柱子上，还留着长江千年来最大一次洪水的泥痕。庙门为红墙黄瓦，门楣正中"老黄陵庙"四个鎏金大字之下，一横排八仙雕像肃然列坐。它是万里长江沿岸历史最悠久、规模最大、保存最完整的纪念大禹治水的古建筑群，也是一座三峡水文变迁的"活档案"。

黄陵庙因治水文化而生。相传远古时三峡洪水泛滥，黄牛神助大禹开江治水，"九载而功成"，此后腾身跃上江南高耸入云的白色崖壁，留下黄牛影像，然后退隐山林。人们称这段峡江为黄牛峡，称有影像的山崖为黄牛岩，并建庙祭祀黄牛神，是为黄牛庙。始建年代历来多说：旧宜昌县志称可上溯至春秋战国，亦有记为汉代、夏代者，长江考古队据出土柱础分析则提出"始建于唐"。三国时诸葛亮率师入蜀途经黄牛峡，"因睹江山之胜，乱石排空"，又听闻禹王与黄牛治水的传说，见庙已毁颓，遂主持重修，并亲撰《黄牛庙记》刻于碑石。此碑为圭形，全文二百二十五字，是庙内最珍贵的文物之一，其中一段写道："古传所载，黄牛助禹开江治水，九载而功成，信不诬也，惜乎庙貌废去，使人太息……仆复而兴之，再建其庙号，目之曰黄牛庙。"唐大中元年（847）复建时改名黄牛祠；唐宣宗大中九年（855）复修扩建，增建禹王殿作为祠庙主体，改祭神牛为主祭大禹。至于何时定名黄陵庙，一说是北宋欧阳修于景祐三年至五年（1036—1038）任夷陵县令时，只信禹王开山之功，不信神牛触石之说，因而改黄牛祠为黄陵庙，并写下《黄牛峡祠》一诗，中有"峡山侵天起青嶂，崖崩路绝无由上。黄牛不下江头饮，行人惟向舟中望"之句，另有"石马系祠前，山鸦嗓丛木……朝朝暮暮见黄牛，徒使行人过此愁"的残句传世。宋代山门前确曾立有两匹石马，欧阳修诗中的"石马系祠前"即是实录。据《东湖县志》第二十六卷所载，明洪武初正式封黄牛庙所祀之牛为"黄陵神"，明永乐年间已有《黄陵神灵应碑记》——可见"黄陵庙"之称在明代以前确已存在。

整座庙宇坐南朝北，环以围墙。主轴线上依次为山门、禹王殿、屈原殿、祖师殿，分别建在逐次升高的台地之上；禹王殿右侧为武侯祠，其后为长生殿；庙后及四周还分布着玉皇阁、黄牛泉井、放生池、白骨塔等。现存建筑群占地约二万零九百八十平方米，建筑面积四千六百八十平方米（一说四千八百平方米）。山门建在海拔七十五点五六米的江边台地上，因外形如"山"字而得名，现存者为清光绪十二年（1886）冬季由宜昌总镇罗缙绅主持重建，穿架式砖木结构，正中高起一道四柱三间牌楼，饰以黑白为主的彩画，庄严凝重。正中阴刻"老黄陵庙"四字，左侧为"南极仙翁倒骑梅花鹿"，右侧为"善才童子降妖龙"——道释二教题材共处一门。其下那组浮雕名"九仙堂"，正中间抱酒壶独饮的是"草仙"，两边是"八仙过海"的八仙。再往中间看，正上方一幅蓝色的是"双凤朝阳"，其下金黄色的是"二龙戏珠"——凤在上而龙在下，民间以为正是光绪年间慈禧太后垂帘听政的写照。两侧三角形彩画自上而下依次为"国色天香""一品清廉""寿居耄耋""富贵吉祥""福到口中""欢天喜地"，右边则为"鹿鹤逢春""四季如春""福在梅梢""人寿吉祥""麟吐玉书"。各道额枋均有精美镂空石雕，牌坊与主体建筑屋脊两端饰有高高翘起的鸱尾，与背后黄牛岭起伏的山势相映成趣。罗缙绅另题门联一副："神佑行人布帆无恙，踵成善举栋宇维新"——说的是往来的过客，文官下轿、武官下马，都要在此祭拜禹王祈求一路平安，而信善之人所捐香火，使庙貌得以维新。山门与戏台组合而建，戏台吊柱雕刻极富巧思；此制源于清嘉庆八年（1803），重庆府事赵田坤见原"敕书楼"中殿宫墙因多年风雨飘摇而崩塌，遂宦囊乐输、倡导重修，将中殿改建为戏台，并撰刻《万世流芳》碑，至今尚存庙中。山门外有青石台阶"三十三步又十八级"——下层十八级，上层三十三级，分别寓意"十八层地狱"与"三十三重天"，衬托出庙宇的高大雄伟；两旁还立有一对通高近三米的镇水神兽，名"獬豹"，集龙头（象征中华民族）、鹿角（象征吉祥）、鱼身（象征水性）、狮背（象征勇猛）、牛脚（象征黄牛神）于一身。如今拾级而上，会看见台阶侧面从低到高标注着长江历次大洪水的水位：一九三一年七十五点零二米，一九九八年七十三点二一米——一段台阶，就是一部缩微的长江洪水史。

禹王殿是庙中最重要的建筑，建在比山门地基高十九米的台地上，占地约四千平方米。大殿重檐歇山顶，穿斗式木结构，八架椽屋，面阔进深均五开间，面阔十八点四四米，进深十六点零二米，柱网面积二百九十五点四平方米，通高十七点七四米（另有记为十六米、约十五米者），平面接近正方形。殿内三十六根主要立柱全部用楠木，明间金柱高达十七点四米，柱径零点七八三米，显示出极高的规格。为增强稳定性，金柱与檐柱均留有侧脚，上下檐普柏枋和阑额的组合断面呈"丁"字形，额枋出头做卷杀；月梁、驼峰用材适中、朴素大方，都是早期遗风。上下檐斗拱共九十六攒，六铺作，规矩整齐，承托飞檐翼角；前、后檐共八根檐柱，上端各置一具龙头鱼身的五彩木雕，奇巧生动；大吻吞脊，上插铁剑二柄以镇龙身。殿内天花板为井口天花，共四十七组，图案是蓝天金龙俯视长江、五彩祥云普照人间；正脊中央置莲花座银质宝瓶，屋面覆黄色琉璃瓦。梁枋上刻有题记："皇明万历戊午孟冬吉旦奉直大夫知夷陵州事豫章吴从哲徵事郎判官将事郎吏目三源候应得本镇善士……同建"；金柱柱础上嵌有俗称"七寸碑"的圆额小碑，横批"永远万世"，竖刻"大明国湖广荆州府归州信士万历四十六年"字样；殿内梁额下方还可看到最后一次重修的题字，有"大清光绪十七年"和红色"钦命"等字样。一九八三年大修前，古建筑专家对大殿做了科学勘测，结论是：该殿明万历四十六年（1618）重建，清雍正、乾隆、光绪年间多次重修，光绪十七年（1891）做过较大规模翻修，但"主要构架和上檐斗拱仍然是明代的遗存……结构简练明快，用材经济合理，是明代末期较好的建筑"，台明、木构架、屋顶"三大组成部分是完整的明代原物"。

大殿正面三块匾额各有来历。下檐悬挂褐底金字"玄功万古"匾，为明崇祯辛巳年（1641）惠王朱常润所题，字体丰润遒劲，边框浮雕二龙戏珠、飞金走彩，表明题写者身份的尊贵，此匾已被编入《中国名匾录》一书。上檐悬挂褐底绿字"砥定江澜"匾，落款"乾隆十四年（1749）岁冬己已觉罗齐格题并书"，字体清秀流转——民间多传为乾隆帝之女爱新觉罗·琪格所书，亦有记为慈禧太后所书者，三说并存。后檐另悬"明德远矣"木匾，褐底蓝字，为出身宜昌县的清代大儒、官至南阳知府的顾嘉衡于光绪甲申年（1884）所书。殿内正中供奉木质禹王坐像，高约四米（一说六米），两旁立盘龙大柱，大禹手持耒耜，背靠汹涌江水浮雕，眉宇间满是忧思。旧时相传，有人向像叩拜时，像会随之起立回敬——虽属传说，却也见出古代工匠技艺之奇。

真正让禹王殿不可替代的，是柱子上的水痕。清同治九年庚午（1870）夏，长江流域连降七天七夜暴雨，暴发自一一五三年有洪水记载以来最大的一次洪水，洪峰秒流量达十一万立方米（一说十万零五千立方米）。洪水冲毁了黄陵庙的山门，却没能冲倒禹王殿——殿内三十六根楠木立柱全部留下了高度一致的澄江泥痕迹，水淹波及阑额，下檐"玄功万古"匾被浸没四十七厘米。立柱因此黑黄分明：未被浸淹的上端为楠木本色，被浸淹过的下端呈淡黄色，澄江泥至今仍敷着在立柱表面的裂缝之中。一九八五年维修时，经科学测量确认这些浸痕为海拔八十一点一六米，是长江三峡一八七〇年特大洪水水位的实物记录；维修中特意保留殿内东北角两根立柱不做油漆，作为水文文物标志予以保护，柱上"老庚午年洪水至此"的字迹至今可辨。屈原殿的情形更惊险：一八六〇年（清咸丰十年）洪水未及此殿，一八七〇年洪水入殿水深一米——若水再涨三步台阶即五十厘米，祖师殿也将进水。正是因为有了这条八十一点一六米的记录，三峡大坝最终将坝高定为一百八十五米、设计流量定为每秒十一万立方米。一九九二年全国人大通过兴建长江三峡工程的决议之前，全国人大、政协与水利专家曾纷纷来此踏勘。

中轴线上的屈原殿与祖师殿（又称佛爷殿）今已不存，仅存遗址。屈原殿建在比禹王殿基高二十七米的台地上，清雍正年间已有，咸丰、同治年间重修过，规制宏伟，施斗拱重檐；祖师殿建在比屈原殿基高十五米的台地上，据《游黄陵庙记》始建于明代——明朝历代皇帝多信奉道教。二殿均毁于抗战：据四十年代曾在庙里当过和尚的杨昌明老人回忆，抗战前庙中主持名宽德，日军占领宜昌后国民党三十军所部退驻庙内，把和尚尽数赶走，禹王殿因存放军粮马料而未被拆毁，屈原殿与佛爷殿则都被拆毁烧了。二殿旧址尚存《丕振家声》《屈氏碑文序》二碑，记清代修建始末。

禹王殿右侧为武侯祠，是后人为纪念诸葛亮重建黄牛庙的功德而修，始建年代有唐代与"不详"二说。现存建筑为清光绪十二年（1886）罗缙绅重建，原本倚靠在禹王殿左侧台明处，正与禹王殿前檐基本成一条线，基础比禹王殿基低七十厘米，后毁于一八七〇年洪水。一九八三年修缮时迁建至今禹王殿后东北角。迁建后的建筑占地约一百六十平方米，面阔十二点二米、三开间，进深十二点五八米、四间，穿架式砖木结构，单檐硬山顶，小青瓦屋面，通高九点六米，正面有前廊，木隔扇装修，掩映在丛丛绿荫中。殿内悬"云霄一羽"匾额，内塑诸葛亮坐像，表现三国故事的壁画惟妙惟肖；祠内后院即长着那株相传诸葛亮手植的铁树。武侯祠之后是长生殿，青砖灰瓦，古朴雅致，与庙内其他建筑的华丽精致迥然不同——它最早是覃家老屋。据《覃氏族谱》记载，黄牛岭覃氏于明代从四川迁到夷陵，以打鱼为生，族中一户富裕人家看中黄陵庙"九龙捧圣"的风水，买下这块地建起祖屋。老屋面阔三间，青砖砌筑，单檐硬山灰瓦顶，马头封火山墙，明间两侧也升起马头墙，同时墙面后退形成入口；室内按中轴对称布局，有前厅、后堂，后堂分上下两层。覃氏族人认为这座老屋同时也是覃氏最早的祠堂，后来一度被用作庙中的普舍堂和积善堂。

庙内那株相传诸葛亮手植的铁树，后人尊称"丞相花"，树龄约一千八百年（一说一千七百年），皮如赤龙之甲，叶似孔雀之翎，古朴俊逸，是我国已知最古老的铁树之一。它在一九八三年葛洲坝截流发电时首度开花，花期延续两月之久；此后又在一九九三年三峡大坝动工前夕、一九九七年大江截流时两度绽放——每一次开花都与长江水利的幸事重合。庙后桔林中还有一股优质矿泉，名黄牛泉（亦称石泉），相传也是诸葛亮亲手开凿，至今聚泉极丰；泉畔有放生池，深广约三米，泉后有白骨塔。黄陵庙左侧二百米处原有玉皇阁，俗称"小庙"，兴建年代至迟在清乾隆年间，光绪十九年（1893）重修，阁基比禹王殿低四十二厘米，一九五四年毁，阁址尚存；阁后尚存一座咸丰四年（1854）的和尚墓，为石质宝塔形。

历代文人在黄陵庙留下的题咏，本身就是一部三峡诗文史。北魏郦道元《水经注》叙此间水路行艰；李白、杜甫、白居易、苏轼、苏辙、黄庭坚、陆游、王士祯等都曾到此。苏轼在《书黄牛庙诗后》中记述了一段奇事：他先与元珍梦中同游峡江一庙，后来政治失利，"日与元珍处，不复忆前梦矣。一日，与元珍溯峡谒黄牛庙。入门惘然，皆梦中所见"。黄庭坚《黔南道中记》与陆游《入蜀记》都记载了黄陵庙的景观以及泉、山、滩的自然情状，陆游于宋孝宗乾道六年（1170）十月初九到此。欧阳修的"潭潭村鼓隔溪闻，楚巫歌舞迎送神"写的是黎民祭祀禹王与神牛的盛况，苏轼的"庙前行客拜且舞，击鼓吹箫屠白羊"写的是庙前的民俗。清代李拔《凿石平江记》记"亲临履勘，设法筹划，群工雨集，万斧雷鸣"，王士祯《蜀道驿程记》亦记此庙。此外还留下张鹏翮、张问陶四代同吟黄陵庙的诗坛佳话。更珍贵的是，庙内大量题记与建筑构件记录着历来的水文情况，由此查明了一六一八年重修以来历次洪水及若干次洪峰水位高度，为三峡水利枢纽工程的设计提供了极其宝贵的水文资料。黄陵庙一九五六年被列为湖北省首批文物保护单位，一九九八年由国家文物局纳入重要省级文物保护单位，二〇〇六年五月二十五日作为明代古建筑被国务院公布为第六批全国重点文物保护单位。

Huangling Temple, anciently the Yellow Ox Temple (Huangniu Miao) or Yellow Ox Shrine (Huangniu Ci), stands at the foot of the Nine Dragons ridge below Huangniuyan Cliff in the middle Xiling Gorge of the Yangtze. The Yellow Ox Gorge is one of the Xiling's "four gorges": the river churns without pause, its waves violent, the banks rising steep and rugged. The temple occupies a square terrace on the south bank. The site is called "Nine Dragons Upholding the Sage" — nine ridges run down from the cliff like nine dragons, and the place their heads converge upon is precisely where the temple stands. It lies about seven kilometers below the Three Gorges Dam site and thirty-five kilometers above the Gezhouba hydro-complex, facing north onto the water with the mountain at its back. A Sacred Ox Plaza fronts it; leaning on the railing one takes in the splendor of the upper and lower Xiling Gorge while anglers sit quietly with lines in the water — unaware that the columns behind them still bear mud from the greatest flood in a thousand years of Yangtze history. The gate is of red wall and yellow tile; beneath four gilded characters reading "Old Huangling Temple," a row of Eight Immortals sits in solemn attendance. It is the oldest, largest, and best-preserved ancient complex along the entire Yangtze dedicated to Yu the Great's taming of the waters — and a living archive of the river's hydrology.

The temple was born of flood-control culture. Legend holds that in remote antiquity, when the Three Gorges were inundated, a yellow ox deity helped Yu the Great open the channel — "nine years, and the work was accomplished" — then leapt onto the towering white cliff wall on the southern bank, leaving its image there before withdrawing into the mountains. The people called this stretch the Yellow Ox Gorge, the cliff the Yellow Ox Cliff, and built a temple to the ox god. Its founding date is variously given: old Yiling gazetteers push it back to the Spring and Autumn and Warring States periods; other accounts say the Han, or even the Xia; the Yangtze Archaeological Team, analyzing excavated column bases, proposed a Tang founding. In the Three Kingdoms period Zhuge Liang led his army into Shu past the gorge and, "beholding the splendor of river and mountain, the jumbled rocks thrusting skyward," heard the legends of Yu and the ox, found the temple ruined, rebuilt it, and composed the Record of the Yellow Ox Temple, cut into stone. The stele is gui-shaped (an elongated pointed tablet) and runs 225 characters — among the temple's most precious relics. It reads in part: "As ancient tradition records, the yellow ox helped Yu open the river and tame the waters, and in nine years the work was accomplished — this is no lie. Alas that the temple's appearance has fallen to ruin, causing a man to sigh... I have restored and raised it up, rebuilt it and given it a name, calling it the Temple of the Yellow Ox." It was rebuilt in the first year of Dazhong, Tang (847) and renamed the Yellow Ox Shrine; in the ninth year of Dazhong (855) it was expanded, the Hall of King Yu added as its principal building, and worship transferred from the ox to Yu the Great. As to when the name Huangling was fixed, one account credits Ouyang Xiu, magistrate of Yiling from 1036 to 1038, who believed only in Yu's labor and dismissed the tale of the ox striking the rock, and who marked the change with his poem "Shrine at the Yellow Ox Gorge": "The gorge hills invade the sky, a blue screen rising; cliffs crumble, the path is cut, no way up. The yellow ox will not come down to drink at the river's head; the traveler can only gaze from his boat." Another fragment survives: "Stone horses tethered before the shrine, crows crying in the thickets... morning after morning, evening after evening, the yellow ox is seen; it only makes the traveler passing here sorrowful." Two stone horses did indeed stand before the gate in the Song — Ouyang's "stone horses tethered before the shrine" is a factual record. The twenty-sixth juan of the Donghu County Gazetteer records that in the early Hongwu reign the ox worshipped here was formally enfeoffed as the "Huangling Deity," and that a Stele of the Huangling Deity's Numinous Responses existed by the Yongle reign — confirming the name predates the Ming.

The complex faces north, ringed by a wall. Its central axis runs: gatehouse, Hall of King Yu, Hall of Qu Yuan, Hall of the Patriarch, each on a successively higher terrace; the Marquis Wu Shrine stands to the right of the Hall of King Yu, the Hall of Longevity behind it; the Jade Emperor Pavilion, Yellow Ox Spring Well, Release Pond, and White Bone Pagoda are scattered about the grounds. The surviving group covers some 20,980 square meters with a floor area of 4,680 square meters (another source gives 4,800). The gatehouse stands on a riverside terrace at 75.56 meters elevation, named for its shape, which resembles the character for "mountain" (shan). The present structure was rebuilt in the winter of the twelfth year of Guangxu (1886) under Luo Jinshen, military commander of Yichang, in post-and-rail brick-and-timber construction, with a four-column, three-bay gate-tower rising at its center, painted chiefly in black and white — solemn and grave. At center the name "Old Huangling Temple" is intaglio-carved; to its left, "The Immortal of the South Pole riding a spotted deer backwards"; to its right, "The Boy Sudhana subduing the dragon" — Daoist and Buddhist subjects sharing a single gate. Below lies a relief group called the "Hall of Nine Immortals": at center the "Grass Immortal" drinking alone, wine pot in arms, flanked by the Eight Immortals crossing the sea. Higher up, a blue panel shows "Phoenixes Facing the Sun," and below it a gold "Two Dragons Playing with a Pearl" — phoenix above, dragon below, popularly read as a portrait of the Guangxu era, when Empress Dowager Cixi ruled from behind the screen. Flanking triangular panels read, from the top: "National Beauty and Heavenly Fragrance," "Purity of the First Rank" — comparing a person to the lotus in its cleanliness — "Longevity to Eighty and Ninety," "Wealth and Auspiciousness," "Fortune Arrives at the Mouth," and "Heaven and Earth Rejoice"; on the right, "Deer and Crane Greet Spring," "Four Seasons Like Spring," "Fortune at the Plum's Tip," "Long Life and Good Fortune," "The Qilin Spits the Jade Book," and again "Heaven and Earth Rejoice." Each lintel carries fine openwork stone carving; the gate-tower and the principal roofs end in high-curving chiwei finials that play against the rolling ridgeline of Yellow Ox Hill behind. Luo Jinshen also composed the couplet: "The god protects the traveler, his sailing cloth unharmed; good works follow in succession, the beams and rafters renewed" — meaning that passing travelers, civil officials descending from their sedans and military officers dismounting, all worshipped Yu here for safe passage, while the incense money of the faithful kept the temple renewed. The gatehouse is combined with an opera stage whose suspended columns are carved with rare ingenuity; this arrangement dates to the eighth year of Jiaqing (1803), when Zhao Tiankun of Chongqing prefecture, seeing the central hall of the former "Imperial Edict Tower" collapsed after years of weather, contributed from his own purse and led a rebuilding, converting the hall into a stage and inscribing a stele, Fragrant for Ten Thousand Generations, still preserved in the temple. Outside the gate runs the stair of "thirty-three steps plus eighteen" — eighteen below, thirty-three above — signifying the Eighteen Hells and the Thirty-Three Heavens, and setting off the temple's height and grandeur. Flanking it stand a pair of water-taming beasts nearly three meters tall, called xiebao: dragon head (for the Chinese nation), deer antlers (auspiciousness), fish body (affinity with water), lion back (ferocity), ox feet (the Yellow Ox deity). Climbing the stair today, one finds its flank marked from low to high with the levels of past floods — 75.02 meters in 1931, 73.21 meters in 1998. A flight of stairs, in miniature, is a history of the river's floods.

The Hall of King Yu is the temple's most important building, standing on a terrace nineteen meters above the gatehouse and occupying roughly 4,000 square meters. It has a double-eave hip-gable roof and column-and-tie timber frame with an eight-rafter span; five bays in both width and depth, 18.44 meters wide and 16.02 meters deep, a column-grid area of 295.4 square meters, and a total height of 17.74 meters (other sources give 16 or about 15 meters) — the plan nearly square. All thirty-six principal columns are nanmu; the central king posts rise 17.4 meters with a diameter of 0.783 meters, a mark of the highest specification. For stability, both king posts and eave columns were set with inward lean; the combined sections of the upper and lower pupaifang and the architrave form a T-shape, with the lintel ends chamfered. Crescent beams and camel-hump braces are modestly scaled and unpretentious — all early-period survivals. The upper and lower eaves carry ninety-six bracket sets in six-tier formation, regular and orderly, bearing the flying eave corners; eight eave columns, front and back, are each topped with a polychrome wood carving of a dragon head on a fish body, strange and lively. The main ridge-swallowing ornament is pierced with two iron swords to pin the dragon's body. The ceiling is a coffered one of forty-seven panels, painted with golden dragons gazing down on the Yangtze against a blue sky and auspicious clouds shining on the world below. At the ridge center sits a silver vase on a lotus throne; the roof is sheathed in yellow glazed tile. An inscription cut into the beams reads: "Auspiciously built in the first month of winter, wuwu year of Wanli, of the Great Ming, by Wu Congzhe, Prefect of Yiling..." A small round-topped tablet nicknamed the "Seven-Inch Stele" is set into a king-post base, with the horizontal phrase "Forever, for ten thousand generations" and a vertical inscription naming a benefactor of Guizhou, Huguang, in the forty-sixth year of Wanli; and beneath the architrave one can still see characters from the last restoration: "seventeenth year of Guangxu" and a red "By Imperial Command." Before the 1983 restoration, experts surveyed the hall and concluded that though rebuilt in the forty-sixth year of Wanli (1618), repeatedly repaired under Yongzheng, Qianlong, and Guangxu, and heavily overhauled in the seventeenth year of Guangxu (1891), "the principal frame and upper-eave bracket sets remain Ming originals; the structure is crisp and economical in its use of material, a fine building of the late Ming" — the platform, timber frame, and roof being "entirely intact Ming fabric."

Three plaques on the hall's facade each have a history. The lower eave carries "Profound Merit for Ten Thousand Ages" in gold on a brown ground, inscribed by Zhu Changrun, Ming Prince of Hui, in the xinsi year of Chongzhen (1641): the script is full and vigorous, the border carved with two dragons playing with a pearl and chased with gold, proclaiming the rank of its donor — the plaque is included in the catalogue Famous Plaques of China. The upper eave carries "Stilling the River's Torrent" in green on brown, signed "Inscribed and written by Gioro Qige, winter of the fourteenth year of Qianlong (1749)," in a graceful flowing hand — popularly attributed to the Qianlong emperor's daughter Aisin Gioro Qige, though some credit Empress Dowager Cixi; three accounts coexist. The rear eave carries a further wooden plaque, "Virtue Shining Far," blue on brown, written in the jiashen year of Guangxu (1884) by Gu Jiaheng, a native of Yiling, a noted Qing scholar who served as prefect of Nanyang. At the hall's center stands a wooden seated statue of Yu the Great, about four meters high (another source says six), flanked by coiled-dragon columns; Yu holds a plough, backed by a relief of surging water, his brow furrowed with care. It was long said that when a worshipper bowed, the figure would rise to return the greeting — a legend, but one that speaks to the marvel of the old craftsmen's art.

What makes the hall irreplaceable, however, is the water on its columns. In the gengwu year, ninth year of Tongzhi (1870), seven days and nights of rain over the Yangtze basin produced the greatest flood recorded since documentation began in 1153, with a peak discharge of 110,000 cubic meters per second (one source gives 105,000). The flood destroyed the temple's gatehouse but could not bring down the Hall of King Yu — all thirty-six nanmu columns retain mud marks at a consistent height; the water reached the architrave and submerged the lower "Profound Merit for Ten Thousand Ages" plaque by forty-seven centimeters. Each column is thus sharply bicolored: the unsubmerged upper portion keeps nanmu's natural hue, the submerged lower portion is pale yellow, with river silt still packed into the surface cracks. During the 1985 restoration, scientific measurement fixed these marks at an elevation of 81.16 meters — a physical record of the 1870 flood level; two columns in the northeast corner were deliberately left unpainted as a hydrological monument, and the inscription "The gengwu-year flood reached here" remains legible. The Hall of Qu Yuan fared worse: the 1860 flood did not reach it, but in 1870 water stood one meter deep inside — and had it risen another three steps, some fifty centimeters, the Hall of the Patriarch would have flooded too. It was on the basis of this 81.16-meter record that the Three Gorges Dam was ultimately set at a height of 185 meters with a design discharge of 110,000 cubic meters per second; before the National People's Congress approved the project in 1992, delegations of legislators, advisers, and hydraulic engineers came here repeatedly to inspect it.

The Hall of Qu Yuan and the Hall of the Patriarch (also called the Buddha Hall) on the central axis no longer survive; only their foundations remain. Qu Yuan's hall stood on a terrace twenty-seven meters above the Hall of King Yu, in existence by the Yongzheng reign and repaired under Xianfeng and Tongzhi, grand in scale with bracket sets and double eaves. The Patriarch's hall rose fifteen meters higher still and, according to the Record of a Visit to Huangling Temple, dated from the Ming, when successive emperors favored Daoism. Both were destroyed in the war: Yang Changming, who served as a monk here in the 1940s, recalled that before the war the abbot was named Kuande; when Japanese forces took Yichang, the Nationalist Thirtieth Army retreated into the temple and drove the monks out. The Hall of King Yu was spared because it stored army grain and fodder; the halls of Qu Yuan and the Buddha were dismantled and burned. Two steles survive at their site — Upholding the Family's Reputation and Preface to the Qu Clan Stele — recording the Qing-era construction.

To the right of the Hall of King Yu stands the Marquis Wu Shrine, built to honor Zhuge Liang's merit in rebuilding the temple; its founding is given variously as the Tang or simply "unknown." The present building was rebuilt by Luo Jinshen in the twelfth year of Guangxu (1886), originally set against the terrace on the left of the Hall of King Yu, its front eave roughly in line with that hall's and its foundation seventy centimeters lower; it was destroyed in the 1870 flood and in 1983 moved to its present site at the northeast corner behind the hall. The relocated building covers about 160 square meters — three bays across a 12.2-meter front, four bays across a depth of 12.58 meters — in post-and-rail brick-and-timber construction with a single-eave flush-gable roof of small grey tiles, 9.6 meters tall, with a front corridor and latticed wood partitions, half-hidden in greenery. Inside hangs the plaque "A Feather Among the Clouds," with a seated statue of Zhuge Liang and vivid murals of Three Kingdoms episodes; in the courtyard behind grows the cycad said to have been planted by Zhuge Liang himself. Behind the shrine is the Hall of Longevity, of grey brick and grey tile, plain and refined in a manner quite unlike the temple's other ornate buildings — it began as the Qin family's old house. The Qin clan of Yellow Ox Ridge, according to their genealogy, moved from Sichuan to Yiling in the Ming and lived by fishing; a well-off household among them, prizing the "Nine Dragons Upholding the Sage" fengshui of the temple site, bought the land and built their ancestral house there. Three bays wide, of grey brick, with a single-eave flush-gable grey-tile roof and horse-head gables — the two central bays rising into horse-head walls as well, the wall face set back to form the entrance — its interior is laid out symmetrically about a central axis, with front hall and rear hall, the latter in two stories. The clan holds that this house was also their earliest ancestral shrine, later serving for a time as the temple's Pushe Hall and Jishan Hall.

The cycad said to have been planted by Zhuge Liang, venerated as the "Minister's Flower," is some 1,800 years old (another source says 1,700) — its bark like the scales of a red dragon, its fronds like a peacock's plumes, austere and spirited, among the oldest cycads known in China. It first bloomed in 1983 when the Gezhouba Dam closed its cofferdam and began generating power, the flowering lasting two months; it bloomed again in 1993 just before work began on the Three Gorges Dam, and again at the great river closure of 1997 — each blossom coinciding with a milestone in the river's harnessing. In the orange grove behind the temple lies a fine mineral spring, the Yellow Ox Spring, also called Stone Spring, said likewise to have been opened by Zhuge Liang's own hands and still abundant; beside it is a release pond some three meters across and deep, and behind that the White Bone Pagoda. Two hundred meters to the temple's left once stood the Jade Emperor Pavilion, colloquially the "Little Temple," built no later than the Qianlong reign and rebuilt in the nineteenth year of Guangxu (1893), its foundation forty-two centimeters below the Hall of King Yu; it was destroyed in 1954 and only its site remains. Behind it survives a monk's tomb of the fourth year of Xianfeng (1854), a stone pagoda.

The verses left at Huangling Temple are themselves a literary history of the Three Gorges. Li Daoyuan's Commentary on the Water Classic describes the difficulty of the passage here; Li Bai, Du Fu, Bai Juyi, Su Shi, Su Zhe, Huang Tingjian, Lu You, and Wang Shizhen all came. Su Shi, in After Copying the Poem on the Yellow Ox Temple, records a curious episode: he and Yuanzhen had once dreamt of visiting a temple on the gorge together; later, when his political fortunes failed, "I spent my days with Yuanzhen and no longer recalled the dream. Then one day, going up the gorge with Yuanzhen to pay respects at the Yellow Ox Temple, I entered the gate and was bewildered — everything was as I had seen in the dream." Huang Tingjian's Record of the Road to Qiannan and Lu You's Record of a Journey into Shu, written on the ninth day of the tenth month of 1170, both describe the temple's setting along with its springs, hills, and rapids. Ouyang Xiu's "Village drums boom across the stream, Chu shamans sing and dance to welcome and send off the gods" evokes the popular worship of Yu and the ox; Su Shi's "Travelers before the temple bow and dance, beating drums, playing pipes, slaughtering white sheep" records the folk customs at the gate. Li Ba of the Qing, in Record of Cutting the Rock to Level the River, writes of coming in person to survey and plan, "workers gathering like rain, ten thousand axes thundering"; Wang Shizhen records the temple in his Record of the Post Road to Shu. The Zhang family produced the cherished instance of four generations — Zhang Penghe to Zhang Wentao — writing poems on the same temple. More valuable still, the many inscriptions and building elements within the temple record hydrological conditions across the centuries, allowing researchers to reconstruct the floods since the 1618 rebuilding and the heights of successive flood crests — data of immense worth to the design of the Three Gorges project. Huangling Temple was listed among Hubei's first batch of protected heritage sites in 1956, elevated to major provincial protection by the National Cultural Heritage Administration in 1998, and on 25 May 2006, as a Ming-dynasty structure, was proclaimed a National Key Protected Heritage Site in the sixth batch by the State Council.
